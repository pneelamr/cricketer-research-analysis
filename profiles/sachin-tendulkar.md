# Sachin Tendulkar: Research Dossier

Taking this as **Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar of India**, who retired in 2013. His son Arjun Tendulkar is a separate, current player; he also made a century on Ranji Trophy debut, in December 2022 [33].

## Identity header

- **Full name:** Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar. ESPNcricinfo lists him as SR Tendulkar [1][3].
- **Born:** 24 April 1973, Bombay (now Mumbai), Maharashtra, India [1].
- **Country / teams:** India (1989–2013); Bombay/Mumbai (1988–2013); Yorkshire (1992); Mumbai Indians in the IPL (2008–2013) [1].
- **Role:** right-hand top-order batter and part-time bowler [1].
- **Batting / bowling style:** right-hand bat; right-arm leg-break and right-arm off-break as the record books list him, and he also bowled medium pace [1]. He bats, bowls and throws right-handed but writes left-handed [1].
- **International debuts:**
  - Test: 15 November 1989 v Pakistan, Karachi (cap 187), aged 16 years 205 days [1][13].
  - ODI: 18 December 1989 v Pakistan, Gujranwala (cap 74) [1].
  - T20I: 1 December 2006 v South Africa (cap 11), his only T20I [1].
- **Status:** retired.
  - Last ODI: 18 March 2012 v Pakistan. He announced his ODI retirement on 23 December 2012 [1].
  - Last IPL season: 2013. He announced his IPL retirement on 26 May 2013, after Mumbai Indians beat Chennai Super Kings in the final [1].
  - Last match of any kind: the Champions League T20 final, October 2013, where he made 14 [1].
  - Last Test: his 200th, v West Indies at the Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai, beginning 14 November 2013. He made 74 in his only innings, finishing 79 short of 16,000 Test runs [1].
- **Captaincy:** India captain in two spells, 1996–97 and 1999–2000 [1].
  - Tests: 25 matches, 4 won, 9 lost, 12 drawn [1][7]. One source gives 5 wins [8].
  - ODIs: 73 matches, 23 won, 43 lost, 1 tied, 6 no result [5][6]. Wikipedia's table gives 2 ties with the same other figures, which would total 74 matches, so the single tie is the consistent version [1].
- **Stats as of:** final career figures. International tables are from cricketweb.net StatsSpider [2], cross-checked against the ESPNcricinfo-sourced Wikipedia infobox (dated 15 November 2013) [1] and ESPNcricinfo and CricketArchive search results [3][4]. Retrieved 16–17 November 2026.

## Career arc

- **First-class debut, December 1988.** Playing for Bombay against Gujarat at the Wankhede Stadium, he made 100 not out. That made him the youngest Indian to score a century on first-class debut. Sources give his age as 15 years 230 days or 15 years 232 days [32].
- **Test debut, November 1989.** He debuted against Pakistan in Karachi at 16 years 205 days. He remained India's youngest international debutant until July 2026 [1][13].
- **Rise through the 1990s.** He was the leading run-scorer at the 1996 World Cup with 523 runs [1]. That year Don Bradman said his batting reminded him of his own [25]. Wisden made him a Cricketer of the Year in 1997, and later named him its Leading Cricketer in the World for 1998 [1].
- **Captaincy, 1996–2000.** His two spells as captain brought 4 wins in 25 Tests and 23 in 73 ODIs. He resigned after a 0–2 home Test series defeat by South Africa in 2000, and Sourav Ganguly took over [1].
- **2003 World Cup.** He scored 673 runs, the most at the tournament. It stood as the record for a single World Cup until 2023 [1][14].
- **Injuries, 2004–05.** Tennis elbow kept him out of the 2004 Champions Trophy and the first two home Tests against Australia that year. He had surgery in May 2005 [28]. The sources disagree on when the problem began: an ESPNcricinfo timeline dates the onset to early 2004, while the ICC dates the diagnosis to August 2004 [28]. Wikipedia notes that his batting became less attacking after the injuries from 2004 onward [1].
- **Sydney, January 2004.** He made 241 not out from 436 balls without hitting a single four through the covers, a widely cited example of restraint [27].
- **IPL, 2008–2013.** He was Mumbai Indians' icon player and captain. In 2010 he scored 618 runs, a season record at the time, and won the Orange Cap and Player of the Tournament [1]. In 2011 he made his maiden T20 hundred, 100 not out off 66 balls [1].
- **First ODI double century, 2010.** He made 200 not out against South Africa, the first double hundred in men's ODIs [1]. He won the ICC's Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy that year [1].
- **2011 World Cup winner.** He scored 482 runs at 53.55 with two hundreds, second at the tournament behind Tillakaratne Dilshan. India beat Sri Lanka in the final [1].
- **100th international hundred, 16 March 2012.** He reached it against Bangladesh in the Asia Cup at Mirpur [1].
- **Farewell, November 2013.** He asked for the two-Test series against West Indies to be played in Kolkata and Mumbai, so that he could finish at his home ground. His 200th and last Test was at the Wankhede [1].

## Playing style and technical profile

- **Compact and orthodox.** In 1996 Bradman said Tendulkar played much the same way he used to, citing his compactness and stroke production [25]. Not everyone agreed. Tony Shillinglaw, author of *Bradman Revisited*, argued that the two were technically different and that Inzamam-ul-Haq's movements were closer to Bradman's [26].
- **Signature shots.** He was known for the straight drive, often played with no follow-through and said to be his favourite, plus a wristy flick to leg and a punch over square [1]. Later in his career he added the paddle sweep, the scoop over short fine leg and the slash over the slips to third man [1]. At 51 he hit an upper-cut six off Jerome Taylor in the 2025 International Masters League final [22].
- **Adapting his game.** Early in his career he often scored hundreds at close to a run a ball. After his injuries from 2004 he became less attacking [1]. The Sydney 241 not out showed the change: he cut out the cover drive and waited for chances to hit to leg [27].
- **Vulnerabilities.** Former Australia coach John Buchanan said he was susceptible to the short ball early in an innings because of his footwork, and that he had a weakness against left-arm pace [1].
- **Pace across formats.** His ODI strike rate of 86.23 against 54.08 in Tests reflects a batter who changed gears by format rather than a natural blocker [2].
- **Bowling.** He bowled medium pace, leg-spin and off-spin, mostly to break partnerships [1]. He took 201 international wickets, including two ODI five-wicket hauls, with a best of 5/32 [2].
- **Fielding.** He took 256 catches in 664 internationals, a solid but unremarkable rate [2].

## Records and milestones

Status checked 17 November 2026.

- **Most Test runs: 15,921.** Still stands. Joe Root is second with 14,278 runs from 169 Tests as of November 2026, 1,643 behind [9]. An undated Gulf News report gives Root 14,216, probably an earlier snapshot.
- **Most ODI runs: 18,426.** Still stands [1].
- **Most international runs: 34,357.** Still stands [1].
- **Most international hundreds: 100** (51 in Tests, 49 in ODIs). Still stands. Kohli had 85 as of January 2026 [16].
- **Most Test hundreds: 51.** Still stands [1].
- **Most ODI hundreds: 49.** Held until 15 November 2023, when Kohli made his 50th in the World Cup semi-final against New Zealand at the Wankhede [14]. Kohli had reached 54 by January 2026 [15].
- **Most Tests (200) and most ODIs (463).** Both still stand [1].
- **Most World Cup runs: 2,278.** Still stands. No men's ODI World Cup has been played since 2023 [18].
- **Most runs in a single World Cup: 673, in 2003.** Held until Kohli passed it during the 2023 tournament [14].
- **First double hundred in men's ODIs:** 200 not out against South Africa in 2010 [1].
- **Most international Player of the Match awards: 76.** Still stands; Kohli is next with 71 [17]. Wikipedia's awards list splits Tendulkar's total as 14 in Tests and 62 in ODIs [17], but the main Tendulkar article says 13 in Tests, which would make 75 [1].
- **Most Test fifties: 68.** Root passed it with his 69th in September 2026 [9].
- **Most fourth-innings runs in Tests.** Root passed Tendulkar in 2026 [10].
- **Youngest Indian international debutant: 16 years 205 days.** Held until 4 July 2026, when Vaibhav Sooryavanshi debuted in a T20I against England at Old Trafford. Most reports give his age as 15 years 99 days [13]. Tendulkar remains India's youngest Test debutant.
- **Most international wins by an Indian:** 72 Test wins and 234 ODI wins [1].

### Awards and honours

- **Indian honours:**
  - Arjuna Award: 1994.
  - Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna: 1997–98.
  - Padma Shri: 1999 [11][12]. The main Wikipedia article's infobox gives 1998, while its awards section, Wikipedia's list of his achievements and Mykhel all give 1999 [1][11][12].
  - Padma Vibhushan: 2008.
  - Bharat Ratna: 2014, the first sportsperson to receive it [1].
- **Wisden:** Cricketer of the Year, 1997; Leading Cricketer in the World, 1998 and 2010 [1].
- **ICC:** Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy, 2010; Hall of Fame, 2019 [1].
- **Other:** Laureus Best Sporting Moment 2000–2020, awarded in 2020; ESPNcricinfo Cricketer of the Generation, 2014; ESPNcricinfo greatest batter of the 21st century, 2026 [1].
- **Honorary roles:** Honorary Group Captain in the Indian Air Force from 2010; Honorary Member of the Order of Australia, year not confirmed [1].

## After playing

- **Parliament.** He was a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha from April 2012 to April 2018 [1]. He donated his salary and allowances, nearly Rs 90 lakh, to the Prime Minister's Relief Fund [1]. His attendance over the six-year term was 8% [1].
- **Ambassador and administrative roles.** He has been a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador for South Asia since November 2013, and was an ambassador for the 2015 World Cup [1]. He sat on the BCCI's Cricket Advisory Committee from 2015 [1]. He captained the MCC in its bicentenary match at Lord's in July 2014, and Wikipedia lists him as Mumbai Indians' mentor for the 2021 IPL [1].
- **Legends cricket.** He captained India Legends to the 2020–21 Road Safety World Series title [1]. On 16 March 2025 he led India Masters to the inaugural International Masters League title in Raipur, beating Brian Lara's West Indies Masters by six wickets. He made 25 in the final [21].
- **Business and academies.**
  - In 2026 he opened the SRT10 Altevol Centre of Excellence in Ahmedabad, his second academy after the SRT10 Global Academy in Navi Mumbai. He has said the aim is to turn India from a sports-loving nation into a sports-playing one [24].
  - In November 2026 he became a strategic investor in Recykal, a Hyderabad waste-management company. The amount was not disclosed [23].

## Off-field and context

- **Port Elizabeth, 2001.** During the second Test against South Africa, match referee Mike Denness gave him a one-Test ban, suspended until 31 December 2001, for "acting on the ball" [29][30].
  - The ICC declined at the time to overrule Denness [29].
  - Wisden records that Denness and the ICC later clarified the offence: Tendulkar had cleaned the seam without telling the umpires, under Law 42.3, rather than tampering with the ball [30].
- **The Ferrari duty waiver, 2002–03.** Fiat gave him a Ferrari 360 Modena in 2002 when he equalled Bradman's 29 Test hundreds [31].
  - The government agreed to waive the import duty and amended customs rules to allow it [31].
  - After criticism and objections from the Delhi High Court, Fiat agreed to pay the duty [31].
- **Injuries.** The 2004–05 tennis elbow is covered in the career arc. A right-foot injury first suffered in 2001 flared up again in 2011 and kept him out of an ODI series [1].
- **Pandora Papers, 2021.** He was named in the Pandora Papers in October 2021. His representatives said his investments were legitimate and fully taxed [1].
