# Rahul Dravid: Research Dossier

Taking this as **Rahul Sharad Dravid of India**, the former Test captain and No. 3 batter who retired in 2012 and later coached India. He is not KL Rahul, the current India batter whose surname is Rahul.

## Identity header

- **Full name:** Rahul Sharad Dravid. Nicknames: "The Wall" and "Mr Dependable" [1].
- **Born:** 11 January 1973, Indore, Madhya Pradesh, India. He grew up in Bangalore (now Bengaluru) [1].
- **Country / teams:**
  - India (1996–2011). He also played for the ICC World XI (1 Test and 3 ODIs in 2005–06) and the Asian Cricket Council XI (1 ODI in 2004–05) [2].
  - Karnataka (1990–91 to 2009–10); Kent (2000); Scottish Saltires (2003) [1][2].
  - IPL: Royal Challengers Bangalore (2008–2010) and Rajasthan Royals (2011–2013), whom he captained [1][2].
- **Role:** right-hand top-order batter, mainly at No. 3 in Tests. He was India's designated wicketkeeper in 73 ODIs [1][3].
- **Batting / bowling style:** right-hand bat; right-arm off-break [1].
- **International debuts:**
  - ODI: 3 April 1996 v Sri Lanka, Singapore (cap 95) [1][3].
  - Test: 20 June 1996 v England at Lord's (cap 207). He made 95 [1][8].
  - T20I: 31 August 2011 v England, Old Trafford (cap 38), his only T20I [1][17].
- **Status:** retired.
  - Announced his ODI and T20I retirement in August 2011. His last ODI was v England at Cardiff on 16 September 2011, where he made 69 off 79 balls [1][3].
  - His last Test was v Australia, beginning 24 January 2012 [1]. He announced his retirement from Test and domestic cricket on 9 March 2012 [1].
  - His last matches of any kind were in the 2013 Champions League T20, where he led Rajasthan Royals to the final [1].
- **Captaincy:** India captain in ODIs from 2000 and in Tests from 2003, first as stand-in for Sourav Ganguly and then full-time, until he resigned in September 2007 [1][9].
  - Tests: 25 matches, 8 won, 6 lost, 11 drawn [9][10].
  - ODIs: 79 matches, 42 won, 33 lost, 4 no result [9][10].
- **Stats as of:** final career figures. Tables are from CricketArchive's career record [2], cross-checked against the ESPNcricinfo-sourced Wikipedia infobox (dated 30 January 2012) [1], BCCI's ODI and Test summaries [3][4] and cricket.com [6]. Retrieved 17 November 2026.

## Career arc

- **Junior and domestic start.** He played for Karnataka at under-15 and under-19 level and made his Ranji Trophy debut in 1990–91 [1][2]. He played two Under-19 Tests for India in 1991–92, with a hundred [2].
- **Debut, 1996.** He made his ODI debut in Singapore in April 1996 and his Test debut at Lord's in June, making 95 [1][8]. His first Test hundred came at the Wanderers, Johannesburg, in January 1997: 148, Man of the Match [5].
- **1999 World Cup.** He was the tournament's leading run-scorer with 461 runs at 65.85, scoring hundreds against Kenya and Sri Lanka on 23 and 26 May [5][8]. His 318 with Sourav Ganguly at Taunton was the first 300-run ODI partnership. Later that year he and Sachin Tendulkar added 331 against New Zealand at Hyderabad, an ODI record for any wicket [1][8].
- **Kolkata, March 2001.** India were following on against Australia when he made 180 and VVS Laxman 281 in a fifth-wicket stand of 376. India won the match [5][21].
- **Peak, 2002–2006.** He scored three hundreds in England in 2002, including 148 at Headingley and 217 at The Oval [5]. At Adelaide in December 2003 he made 233 and 72 not out to win the Test [5][22]. At Rawalpindi in April 2004 he made 270, his highest score, as India won their first Test series in Pakistan [1][5]. In December 2004 at Chittagong he became the first batter to score Test hundreds in all ten Test-playing countries [20]. He was the ICC Cricketer of the Year and Test Player of the Year in 2004 [1].
- **ODI wicketkeeper.** To fit an extra batter into the side he kept wicket in 73 ODIs. He averaged 44.23 with four hundreds in those matches, above his overall ODI average of 39.16 [3]. At the 2003 World Cup, where India were runners-up, he scored 318 runs at 63.60 and made 16 dismissals [1].
- **Captaincy, 2003–2007.**
  - First Test series win in the West Indies since 1971 (1–0, 2006) and first Test win in South Africa (Johannesburg, December 2006) [9][11].
  - First Test series win in England since 1986 (1–0, 2007) [1][9].
  - India went out in the group stage of the 2007 World Cup after losing to Bangladesh, their worst showing since 1979 [1].
  - He resigned in September 2007. MS Dhoni took over the white-ball sides and Anil Kumble the Test side [1].
- **Late flourish, 2011.** In England, while India lost the series 4–0, he made 461 runs at 76.83 with three hundreds and was India's Man of the Series. That was more than 24% of India's runs [19]. He then played the one-off T20I and the ODI series, and retired from white-ball internationals at Cardiff [1].
- **Retirement, 2012.** He retired from Tests after the 2011–12 tour of Australia. At the time only Tendulkar had scored more Test runs [1][35].
- **Coaching, 2015–2024.**
  - Head coach of India A and India Under-19 from 2015. The Under-19s were runners-up at the 2016 World Cup and won the 2018 edition [1].
  - Head of Cricket at the National Cricket Academy from July 2019 [1].
  - India head coach from November 2021 to June 2024 [1]. India reached the semi-finals of the 2022 T20 World Cup, lost the 2023 World Test Championship final and the 2023 ODI World Cup final, both to Australia, then won the 2024 T20 World Cup, beating South Africa at the Kensington Oval in Barbados [1][27].
  - Verdicts on the tenure were mixed before the 2024 title. After the 2022 semi-final exit, Wisden wrote that it had "thrown up more questions than answers" [28].

## Playing style and technical profile

- **Defence first.** His method was built on protecting his wicket. Wisden's Alan Lee called it "painstaking" and praised its "understated, old-fashioned grace" [8]. Cheteshwar Pujara, who succeeded him at No. 3, described his forward defence as the model he copied, with a straight bat at its centre [25].
- **Waiting for the ball.** Mukul Kesavan argued that his defence was "extravagant rather than compact", and that the whole technique rested on making sure the ball had pitched before he committed [24].
- **Defend, then punish.** Sanjay Manjrekar called him a defensive batter whose forte was "to defend the good balls and hit the loose ones for four". Once past fifty he did not change gear, even when his partner was scoring much faster [26].
- **Scoring shots.** Manjrekar singled out the pull as "a superb instinctive stroke against fast bowling" that repeatedly got him out of tight Test situations [26]. Before his 1996 debut England's scouting reportedly said he struggled to work the straight ball to leg; Wisden says the tour quickly disproved this [8].
- **Better away than at home.** He averaged 53.03 away (7,690 runs) against 51.35 at home; ESPNcricinfo gives the home figure as 51.36. At his retirement only Tendulkar had scored more away Test runs [7].
- **The No. 3.** He scored 10,524 Test runs at No. 3 at 52.88, with 28 hundreds in 219 innings. He was the first batter to reach 10,000 Test runs at No. 3; Kumar Sangakkara later finished with more, 11,679 [7][8].
- **Stamina.** He faced 31,258 balls in Tests and spent 44,152 minutes at the crease, both records [15][16]. The 270 at Rawalpindi lasted 740 minutes [15].
- **One-day adaptation.** His ODI strike rate of 71.23 was modest for his era, but he lifted it to 75.42 as captain while averaging 42.19 [2][3]. His one T20I showed a range he rarely used: from 13 off 16 balls he hit Samit Patel for three consecutive sixes, finishing with 31 off 21 [17][18].
- **Fielding.** A specialist slip, he took 210 Test catches, the record for a non-wicketkeeper until July 2025 [12].

## Records and milestones

Status checked 17 November 2026.

- **Most balls faced in Tests: 31,258.** Still stands. Tendulkar is second with 29,437 [15].
- **Most time at the crease in Tests: 44,152 minutes.** Still stands [16].
- **Most Test catches by a non-wicketkeeper: 210.** Held until 11 July 2025, when Joe Root took his 211th at Lord's [12][13].
- **First batter to score Test hundreds in all ten Test-playing countries.** He completed the set with 160 against Bangladesh at Chittagong in December 2004 [5][20]. There are now twelve Test-playing countries.
- **First to 10,000 Test runs at No. 3.** He finished with 10,524. Sangakkara later passed his total [7].
- **Test run rank: fifth, with 13,288.** He was second to Tendulkar at his retirement [35]. Ponting and Kallis passed him later, and Root passed all three in July 2025 [14]. Kallis's total is 13,289, one run more [14].
- **ODI partnerships:**
  - 318 with Ganguly v Sri Lanka at Taunton, 1999: the first 300-run partnership in ODIs [1][8].
  - 331 with Tendulkar v New Zealand at Hyderabad, 1999: the ODI record for any wicket, held for about 15 years [1].
- **Test partnerships:** 376 for the fifth wicket with Laxman at Kolkata, 2001, an Indian record for that wicket [21]; 410 for the first wicket with Virender Sehwag at Lahore, 2006 [1].
- **Hundreds in four consecutive Test innings,** in 2002 [1].
- **Five Test double hundreds:** 200*, 217, 222, 233 and 270 [5].
- **Leading run-scorer at the 1999 World Cup:** 461 runs at 65.85 [8].
- **Seventh-highest ODI run-scorer at his retirement, and third among Indians,** per BCCI [3]. His current position was not checked.

### Awards and honours

- **Indian honours:** Arjuna Award, 1998; Padma Shri, 2004; Padma Bhushan, 2013 [1].
- **Wisden:** Cricketer of the Year, 2000 [1].
- **ICC:** Cricketer of the Year and Test Player of the Year, 2004; captain of the ICC Test Team of the Year, 2006; Hall of Fame, July 2018, the fifth Indian inducted [1].
- **BCCI:** Col. C. K. Nayudu Lifetime Achievement Award, presented at the Naman Awards in New Delhi on 15 March 2026 [34].
- **Other:** first non-Australian to deliver the Bradman Oration, Canberra, December 2011; Don Bradman Award, 2012 [1].

## After playing

- **Coaching.** See the career arc for the India A, Under-19, NCA and senior India roles [1].
- **Rajasthan Royals.** He was the Royals' mentor in 2014–15, and returned as head coach for IPL 2025 [1][29]. On 30 August 2025 the franchise announced he was leaving: he had been offered a broader role and turned it down [29].
- **European T20 Premier League.** In May 2026 he was announced as owner of the Dublin Guardians in the ETPL, Europe's first ICC-sanctioned multi-country franchise league [30][31]. Reports differ on his stake: the Irish Times describes him as part of an Indian consortium, while the ETPL and RTE describe him as owner [30][31]. R Ashwin captains the side, and the league was scheduled to start on 26 August 2026 [30].
- **England job.** In July 2026 reports named him among candidates to succeed Brendon McCullum as England's Test coach, while noting he was not actively seeking a full-time post [32]. Stephen Fleming was appointed instead [33]. Dravid told BBC Sport he would consider coaching if "good opportunities come up" [32].

## Off-field and context

- **Multan declaration, 2004.** As stand-in captain against Pakistan, he declared at 675/5 with Tendulkar on 194 not out [23].
  - India won by an innings and 52 runs [23].
  - In his autobiography Tendulkar wrote that he was "shocked" by the decision [23].
  - Coach John Wright later wrote that he should have persuaded Dravid to declare earlier [23].
  - Dravid said there was no lasting rift and later admitted he regretted the timing: "We respect each other too much to let it linger on" [23].
- **Leaving the captaincy, 2007.** He met BCCI president Sharad Pawar on 13 September 2007 to resign, months after the World Cup exit and soon after the series win in England [1].
- **Personal.** He married Vijeta Pendharkar, a surgeon from Nagpur, on 4 May 2003. They have two sons, Samit (born 2005) and Anvay (born 2009) [1].
- **Social work.** He has supported UNICEF, an AIDS awareness campaign and the Children's Movement for Civic Awareness [1].
