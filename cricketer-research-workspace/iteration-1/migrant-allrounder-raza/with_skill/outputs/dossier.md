# Sikandar Raza — research dossier

Prepared 16 November 2026. Your angle is cricketers who played for a country they were not born in, so the dossier keeps the standard structure but weights the career arc, the context section and the ratings toward the migration-and-eligibility story.

**Identity check.** Taken as Sikandar Raza Butt, the Pakistan-born Zimbabwe all-rounder. No other international cricketer of this name turned up in searches. Spelling follows ESPNcricinfo ("Sikandar Raza"); "Sikander Raza" appears in some South African and Nepali reports.

## Identity header

- **Full name:** Sikandar Raza Butt; known as Sikandar Raza
- **Born:** 24 April 1986, Sialkot, Punjab, Pakistan (age 40)
- **Country / teams:** Zimbabwe (2013–present). Zimbabwe domestic: Northerns (2006/07–2008/09), Southern Rocks (2009/10–2010/11), Mashonaland Eagles (2011/12–2016/17), Matabeleland Tuskers (2017/18–2019/20). Franchise and overseas: Chittagong Vikings (BPL, 2017–19), Trinbago Knight Riders (CPL 2020), Lahore Qalandars (PSL 2023–25), Punjab Kings (IPL 2023–24), Dambulla Sixers (LPL 2023–), Dubai Capitals (ILT20), Northamptonshire (T20 Blast 2024), Paarl Royals (SA20 2026), Worcestershire (T20 Blast 2026), Chitwan Rhinos (Nepal Premier League 2026); earlier Montreal Tigers (Global T20 Canada 2018) and Tshwane Spartans (Mzansi Super League 2018). A Seattle Orcas (MLC) profile exists but that stint was not verified.
- **Role:** batting all-rounder — middle-order batter and off-spinner
- **Batting / bowling style:** right-hand bat; right-arm off-break (ESPNcricinfo also lists leg-break; Worcestershire describe him as a "mystery spinner")
- **International debuts:** ODI 3 May 2013 v Bangladesh (Zimbabwe cap 116); T20I 11 May 2013 v Bangladesh (cap 37); Test 3 September 2013 v Pakistan, Harare Sports Club (cap 89)
- **Status:** active. Zimbabwe T20I captain (appointed late 2023); stand-in ODI captain September–October 2026 while regular captain Richard Ngarava recovers from a back injury. Last Test 20 October 2025 v Afghanistan; not in the November 2026 Test squad, and no formal Test retirement announced. Told PTI on 15 October 2026 that he will retire from international cricket after the 2027 ODI World Cup, which Zimbabwe co-hosts.
- **Stats as of:** 12 November 2026, ESPNcricinfo figures as carried by Wikipedia's infobox, cross-checked on 16 November 2026 against cricket.com.au (identical figures). His most recent international was 1 November 2026, so the tables are current. ESPNcricinfo and HowSTAT refused automated fetches, so their pages were not read directly — see the notes under the tables.

## Career arc

- **Sialkot to Harare.** Born in Sialkot to a Punjabi-speaking family of Kashmiri origin. Spent three years at Pakistan Air Force College, Lower Topa, intending to become a fighter pilot, and failed the mandatory eyesight test. His family emigrated to Zimbabwe in 2002 (Wikipedia) or 2003 (Al Jazeera, Zimbabwe Independent); the sources do not agree and none gives the reason for the move.
- **A student cricketer, not a prospect.** Took a BSc in software engineering at Glasgow Caledonian University, playing club cricket in Scotland (three T20 hundreds for Weirs CC in 2006) and at Alexandra Sports Club in Harare during holidays. Former Zimbabwe coach Stephen Mangongo recalled him as "simply an ordinary player when he started club cricket" who stood out for energy and willingness to learn. He described his own approach as playing "just for fun" and took a gap year in 2009 to try cricket seriously: "I got lucky, I guess."
- **Domestic career while ineligible.** First-class debut 12 April 2007 for Northerns in the Logan Cup; leading run-scorer in the 2010 Stanbic Bank T20; a 161-run opening stand with Chamu Chibhabha got him into Zimbabwe's preliminary 2011 World Cup squad, but he was not a citizen and could not be picked.
- **Citizenship and debut.** Zimbabwean citizenship was granted in September 2011. ODI debut in May 2013 against Bangladesh at 27, batting at No. 3 for three runs; T20I debut eight days later; Test debut in September 2013 against Pakistan, the country of his birth, scoring 60 in a 127-run stand with Malcolm Waller, then dropped when Brendan Taylor returned.
- **2014–15: first peak with the bat.** Maiden ODI hundred, 141 v Afghanistan in July 2014, opening the batting, in a 224-run stand with Hamilton Masakadza that remains Zimbabwe's record ODI opening partnership (whether it still stands was not re-checked). Played the 2015 World Cup. Stood in as T20I captain for a 10-run win over India in 2015.
- **2017–18: the Test all-rounder.** Maiden Test century (127) in the one-off Test in Sri Lanka in 2017, a match Sri Lanka won chasing 388. Was Zimbabwe's man of the match in the decider as they won the ODI series 3–2 in Sri Lanka, their first away series win since 2009. Against West Indies in 2017 he scored 80-plus in both innings and took a five-wicket haul in the same Test; only Jacques Kallis had done that before. Player of the Tournament at the 2018 World Cup Qualifier (319 runs, 15 wickets), but Zimbabwe failed to qualify and he was left out of the home tri-series after telling the audience the team had "broken the hearts of 15 million Zimbabweans" and criticising the ten-team World Cup.
- **2020–21: a spinner's Test high and an illness.** 7/113 from 43 overs against Sri Lanka in January 2020, the second-best Test innings figures by a Zimbabwean. 100th ODI in March 2020. Out from April 2021 with a bone-marrow infection, amid reports (later played down) that it was cancerous; back for the Bangladesh ODIs in July 2021.
- **2022: reinvention at 36.** Player of the Tournament at the T20 World Cup Qualifier B in Zimbabwe, taking 4/8 in the final against Netherlands. In August 2022 he made 135* and 117* in successive successful chases against Bangladesh, then 115 off 95 against India, becoming the first player to score three ODI hundreds in successful chases in a single calendar month. Played the T20 World Cup in Australia. ICC Men's T20I Cricketer of the Year nominee on 735 runs and 25 wickets in 24 matches.
- **2023–24: franchise cricketer and captain.** Zimbabwe's fastest ODI hundred, 102* off 54 against Netherlands at the 2023 World Cup Qualifier, in a tournament Zimbabwe again failed to get through. First Zimbabwean to score an IPL fifty (57 off 41 for Punjab Kings). PSL title with Lahore Qalandars in 2023. Appointed Zimbabwe's T20I captain in late 2023 with Craig Ervine keeping the ODI and Test jobs. Nominated for the ICC T20I award again in 2023 (515 runs at a strike rate of 150, 17 wickets) and 2024. Five consecutive T20I fifties in January 2024, a world record; 133* off 43 balls against Gambia in October 2024, the first T20I century by a Zimbabwean.
- **2025: the 24 hours that define him.** Scored 60 on day three of the Trent Bridge Test against England, flew overnight to Lahore, arrived ten minutes before the toss and hit 22* off 7 to win the PSL final for Lahore Qalandars on 25 May; named all-rounder of the tournament. A Wisden headline on 3 September 2025 reports a 39-year-old all-rounder becoming the ICC's No. 1 ODI all-rounder, which points to Raza, though the article body was not read. Last Test in October 2025 against Afghanistan.
- **2026, aged 39–40.** First Zimbabwean to play in the SA20, four days after his 13-year-old brother died. Captained Zimbabwe at the T20 World Cup: beat Australia by 23 runs in Colombo on 13 February, reached the Super 8, and was Player of the Match in a losing cause against South Africa (73 and 3/29). Regained the ICC No. 1 T20I all-rounder ranking with a career-best 328 rating points, entering the all-time top ten. Stand-in ODI captain against Australia and in the UAE; 106* off 56 in a 0–3 T20I series loss to Afghanistan; left out of the Tests. Now captaining Chitwan Rhinos in Nepal, with the 2027 home World Cup set as his finish line.

## Playing style and technical profile

- **A chase-and-tempo batter, not an accumulator.** Bats in the middle order (Nos. 4–6; opened in 2014). The ODI record is built on finishing chases: 141 in a successful chase in 2014, 135* chasing 304 and 117* in the next match in 2022, and 102* off 54 in 2023. Against India in 2022 he moved from 46 to 100 in 36 balls. Ricky Ponting on that form: "He plays with a youthful exuberance — it looks like he's 26 again."
- **Late-career strike-rate rise.** T20I strike rate of 136.51 overall, but 150-plus in 2023 (ICC nomination citation), five successive T20I fifties in January 2024 and the 43-ball 133* in October 2024 came at 37–38. Al Jazeera's tally in March 2024: almost 2,500 runs and 71 wickets across ODIs and T20Is since January 2022. The T20I average of 26 reflects a career of coming in with Zimbabwe under pressure rather than a technical limitation.
- **Test batting:** average 34 with one hundred and eleven fifties in 22 Tests, so a reliable 50s-maker rather than a converter; the two 80s against West Indies in 2017 and 60s on debut and at Trent Bridge are typical.
- **Off-spin: containment first, wickets in bursts.** ESPNcricinfo lists both off-break and leg-break and English counties describe him as a mystery spinner, so he varies his action and release. In T20Is the numbers are those of a frontline spinner: 116 wickets at 25.41 and an economy of 7.08 across 147 matches, with 4/8 (including a maiden) in a tournament final. In ODIs he is a fifth-bowler option (101 wickets at 44.00, economy 4.87). In Tests he has been used as a stock bowler when needed, most obviously the 43-over 7/113 in 2020, but the average of 44.55 shows that is not his primary job.
- **Fielding:** 137 international catches. No qualitative coach or commentator assessment of his fielding was found in this research, so no stronger claim is made.
- **Temperament and leadership.** Outspoken when he thinks it matters (the 2018 speech cost him a place in the next squad). Plays through adversity: returned from the 2021 illness within three months; played in the SA20 four days after a bereavement; made the Trent Bridge-to-Lahore trip in 2025. Team-mate Sean Williams: "He has turned into one hell of a player ... he is a seriously good human being." Raza on his own drive: "The whole idea was just to see who was ahead of me."

## Off-field and context

**The migration story, for your piece.** Raza's case is unusual among cross-border cricketers in that he was not a talent import. He arrived in Zimbabwe as a teenager with his family (2002 or 2003; the reason is not documented in the sources used), had already been steered away from a military flying career by an eyesight test, and was by his own account a hobby cricketer while studying software engineering in Scotland. He played provincial cricket in Zimbabwe for four seasons as a non-citizen, was named in a preliminary World Cup squad he could not actually join, and only became eligible when citizenship was granted in September 2011; the debut followed in 2013 at 27. His first Test was against Pakistan. He has since built the Pakistani leg of his career in the PSL, where the 2025 final in Lahore made him a crowd favourite in the country of his birth, and Al Jazeera's 2024 profile is headlined on the idea of the "Pakistan-born cricketer who rules Zimbabwean hearts". No direct quote from Raza on choosing Zimbabwe over Pakistan was found in the sources read; Al Jazeera paraphrases that "the once shy part-time Pakistani cricketer is now proud to call himself a Zimbabwean". If the piece needs his own words on identity, that is the gap to fill with an interview source.

**Captaincy.** Stand-in T20I captain in 2015; T20I captain since late 2023 (Craig Ervine kept the ODI and Test captaincy, later passed to Richard Ngarava); stand-in ODI captain from September 2026. Four matches as Mashonaland Eagles captain in 2012. Captaincy win–loss figures were not obtainable.

**Disputes.** Left out of the 2018 home tri-series by Zimbabwe Cricket, along with Brendan Taylor, after the public remarks that followed the 2018 Qualifier. No sanctions found.

**Health.** Bone-marrow infection, April–July 2021.

**Bereavement.** His brother Muhammad Mahdi died in January 2026 aged 13; he played for Paarl Royals four days later.

**Philanthropy.** Al Jazeera (2024) reports work with cancer foundations and orphanages, funded partly from T20 league earnings.
