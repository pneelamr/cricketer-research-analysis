# Sikandar Raza: Research Dossier

Prepared 16 November 2026 for a piece on cricketers who have represented a country other than the one they were born in.

Confidence tags used below: **[solid]** = multiple independent or primary sources agree; **[single]** = one source only; **[conflict]** = sources disagree, see section 15; **[memory]** = from general knowledge, not verified in this research, check before print.

---

## 1. The short version

Sikandar Raza Butt was born in Sialkot, Pakistan, in April 1986, wanted to be a Pakistan Air Force fighter pilot, failed the eyesight test, and emigrated to Zimbabwe with his parents as a teenager around 2002. He studied software engineering in Glasgow, played Scottish club cricket, came back to Harare, and only committed to cricket full-time after giving it a one-year trial in 2009. Zimbabwe's selectors wanted him by 2011 but the citizenship process dragged; he says he nearly quit when told he might have to wait four more years. He debuted in May 2013 and has since become Zimbabwe's most-capped international (314 caps on 19 July 2026, passing Hamilton Masakadza), their T20I captain, a two-time PSL champion with Lahore Qalandars, the first Zimbabwean to score an IPL fifty and a T20I hundred, a three-time ICC T20I Cricketer of the Year nominee (2022, 2023, 2024), and, at 40, the oldest man to score a T20I century (106* v Afghanistan, Sharjah, late October 2026).

What makes him unusual for your piece: he did not migrate for cricket, he refuses to disown either country ("I am a born Pakistani and a product of Zimbabwe Cricket"), he has become a mainstream celebrity in his birth country through the PSL while repeatedly turning down the idea of playing for it, and his defining international moment was beating Pakistan by one run at the 2022 T20 World Cup. He has announced the 2027 ODI World Cup, co-hosted by Zimbabwe, will be his last tournament.

## 4. The migration and citizenship story (the core of your piece)

**Why he left Pakistan is not cricket.** Every account agrees the sequence was: pilot ambition, failed vision test, family emigration, university in Scotland, cricket almost as an afterthought. He has said he was ready to do a master's degree and only gave cricket a one-year trial in 2009. That distinguishes him from the "cricket migrant" archetype (the South African who moves to England at 20 with a county contract in hand). The reason the family chose Zimbabwe is not stated in anything I could retrieve; this is the first question to put to him or his family if you get access.

**The eligibility wait.** By 2011 his domestic numbers were good enough that Zimbabwe's selectors wanted him in an A-team training squad, but he held no Zimbabwean citizenship. A Herald piece from around November 2011 ("Can Raza Butt for Zim?") describes selectors waiting on the Ministry of Home Affairs to regularise his status. A Zimbabwean profile site says the authorities initially refused on the grounds he was born in Pakistan [single, low confidence]. Wikipedia and Wisden place the grant of citizenship in September 2011, but that sits awkwardly with the Herald still reporting an unresolved application in November 2011, and with Raza's own April 2013 comment that he had been told he faced four more years of waiting and had nearly quit. A Sunday News interview has him obtaining his national ID through his father and debuting ten days later. The honest summary: the paperwork took roughly two years from the first selectorial interest, the exact date and legal route are unclear, and he was close to giving up. Treat the September 2011 date as unconfirmed.

**Whether he renounced Pakistani nationality is unknown.** Zimbabwe did not generally permit adult dual citizenship before its 2013 constitution, and even now extends it only to citizens by birth; Pakistan's dual-nationality arrangements do not, to my knowledge, include Zimbabwe [memory]. If he naturalised in 2011-13 it is plausible he had to give up his Pakistani passport, but no source I found says so. This is a factual point worth putting to him directly, because it changes the emotional weight of "I am a born Pakistani".

**The Antum Naqvi echo.** Zimbabwe's next Pakistani-heritage player, the Belgian-born, Australian-raised batter and commercial pilot Antum Naqvi, was called up for the India T20Is in July 2024 but could not play because his citizenship documentation was incomplete; a Zimbabwean paper had already headlined him "the next Sikandar Raza". It shows the pathway Raza opened and that Zimbabwe's eligibility bureaucracy remains slow a decade on.

**Visa geopolitics.** As a Pakistan-born player he was the subject of a January 2026 rumour that India had refused him a visa for the T20 World Cup. It was false (fact-checked by cricket.one, and dismissed as "fake" by Zimbabwean journalist Adam Theo), and he played Super 8 games in India. But the rumour only had legs because Pakistan-origin USA players really were facing delays, which is a useful illustration of how birth country follows a migrant cricketer around.

## 5. In his own words: identity quotes

Verified against the outlet that carried them unless marked secondhand.

- **On birth and belonging (X fan Q&A, 3 August 2024, asked whether he would play for Pakistan to fix its middle order):** "I am a born Pakistani and a product of Zimbabwe Cricket. I will only and ever represent Zimbabwe." / "[Zimbabwe] spent time and money on me and I am only trying to repay their faith." / "Whatever I achieve will never even get close to repaying it." / "Zim is mine and am theirs fully." (Express Tribune and CricketPakistan, both 4 August 2024.)
- **On holding both identities (BBC Sport, November 2022, after the Perth win, as reprinted by the Financial Gazette; secondhand paraphrase):** he still regards himself as a proud Pakistani and a proud Zimbabwe cricketer; neither country has taken his identity away from him; Pakistan recognises that Zimbabwe developed him and Zimbabwe respects his Pakistani roots. He described a cousin in Pakistan crying over a video of the match while her brother laughed because he had backed Raza. The original BBC article is the one to pull for exact wording.
- **On Zimbabwe's heartbreak (World Cup Qualifier award speech, Harare, March 2018):** "This trophy is a painful reminder of the dreams that we had, of the 15 million dreams we crushed." He had framed the whole tournament as a mission to give "15 million Zimbabweans" something to cheer. He also criticised the ten-team World Cup format from the podium.
- **On the pilot who never was:** ESPNcricinfo profile headline (2016): "In my heart I wanted to be a fighter pilot." Later ESPNcricinfo line (2022): "I may not be a fighter pilot, but I am a fighter within myself."
- **On cricket as refuge (Al Jazeera, March 2024):** "Cricket has always been a passion for me, a source of happiness." On his life before: "My early life in Pakistan was studies, studies and studies."
- **On the Perth win over Pakistan (October 2022):** "Can't tell how proud I'm of this bunch." He publicly thanked Ricky Ponting for "a little push" after Ponting had talked him up before the tournament.
- **On comparisons:** headline from a CricTracker interview: "I never like people mentioning me as Shakib Al Hasan of Zimbabwe."
- **On the 2018 contract row:** "I am extremely delighted with the news and thanks to ZC who quickly sorted out my issues."
- **On the 2027 home World Cup (October 2026):** it is "a blessing"; on un-retiring: "It will not happen. That's not me."

## 6. How Zimbabwe and Pakistan have received him

**Zimbabwe.** Al Jazeera's Harare-based correspondent Enock Muchinjo (March 2024) describes sold-out crowds singing a football chant adapted to Raza's name, credits him with reviving interest in cricket in a football country, and calls him the face of Zimbabwe cricket. Teammate Sean Williams: he "has turned into one hell of a player", and praised his faith and charity work (cancer foundations, orphanages, visits to sick children on short trips home). Former coach Stephen Mangongo remembered his energy and eagerness when he first turned up at club level. A Chronicle headline after one of his big performances read "Raza, you patriot: Pakistani immigrant proves to be more Zimbabwean than Zimbos", and the Zimbabwe Independent ran an opinion piece titled "How a Pakistani boy became a cricket icon and national hero in Zimbabwe" (I could not retrieve either body text; both are worth pulling). The counterpoint: ZC terminated his contract in 2018 and left him out of a home tri-series, and the 2011-13 citizenship delay shows the state was slower to claim him than the fans were.

**Pakistan.** He is a star of the PSL with Lahore Qalandars (titles in 2023 and 2025, the 2025 one sealed with his own bat after the Trent Bridge dash), and Pakistani outlets (Express Tribune, Dawn, Geo, CricketPakistan) cover him as one of their own, down to his picks of favourite Pakistani players (Abdullah Shafique, Shaheen Afridi). Pakistani fans periodically ask him to switch; he has always declined. The one-run defeat in Perth produced mixed reactions in his birth country, which he has spoken about with humour rather than grievance. [memory, verify before use: the Perth match became entwined with the "Pak Bean" meme about a Pakistani Mr Bean impersonator who had toured Zimbabwe, and President Emmerson Mnangagwa tweeted a "send the real Mr Bean" taunt after the win.]

**Observers elsewhere.** Ricky Ponting: Raza "plays with a youthful exuberance." The ICC has named him in its T20I Team of the Year (2022, 2023) and ODI Team of the Year (2022) and shortlisted him three years running for T20I Cricketer of the Year.

## 12. Flashpoints and setbacks

## 13. Where he is now and what is next

As of mid-November 2026 he is 40, Zimbabwe's T20I captain and senior statesman, fresh from a record-setting T20I hundred in Sharjah, with 331 or so international caps. His stated finish line is the 2027 ODI World Cup in Zimbabwe, South Africa and Namibia; Zimbabwe v India on 24 October 2027 is scheduled to open the new Mosi-oa-Tunya stadium in Victoria Falls. He has ruled out carrying on into a possible Zimbabwe WTC entry. Zimbabwe are guaranteed a 2028 T20 World Cup place, which he will not be part of on current plans.

## 14. Angles and comparisons for your piece

- **The non-mercenary migrant.** He left Pakistan for family reasons at 15-16, with no cricket pathway, and chose cricket at 23. Contrast with players who moved for a contract.
- **The player who will not pick a side.** His formula ("born Pakistani, product of Zimbabwe Cricket") is unusually explicit; most migrant cricketers either go quiet on the birth country or perform total conversion.
- **The homecoming via franchise.** The PSL has given him a Pakistani public life his national career could not; the May 2025 Trent Bridge-to-Lahore dash is the perfect set piece.
- **Playing the birth country.** Perth 2022 (one run) and captaining against Pakistan at home in 2024.
- **The state vs the fans.** Fans and the dressing room claimed him before the Home Affairs ministry did; Antum Naqvi's 2024 delay suggests the process has not sped up.
- **Birth country as liability.** The 2026 India visa rumour.
- **Succession.** He is explicitly handing over to Bennett, Nyamhuri, Ngarava.
- **Peers for comparison [memory, verify each]:** Kevin Pietersen (South Africa to England), Jofra Archer (Barbados to England), Devon Conway (South Africa to New Zealand), Imran Tahir (Pakistan to South Africa), Fawad Ahmed and Usman Khawaja (Pakistan to Australia), Eoin Morgan (Ireland to England), Gary Ballance (Zimbabwe to England and back to Zimbabwe). Among these, Tahir, Ahmed and Khawaja are the closest Pakistan-born parallels; none has Raza's combination of captaincy, longevity and simultaneous celebrity in the birth country.

**People worth approaching:** Enock Muchinjo (Al Jazeera, Harare); Sean Williams; Hamilton Masakadza; Stephen Mangongo; Givemore Makoni (ZC); Pommie Mbangwa (interviewed him after the 2018 speech); Adam Theo (Zimbabwean cricket journalist); Weirs CC in Glasgow.
