# Virat Kohli vs Steven Smith: who is the better Test batter?

## Question

Who is the better Test batter, Virat Kohli (India) or Steven Smith (Australia)? ODIs and T20Is are covered as cross-format context. **Stats as of 16 Nov 2026.**

- **Headline figures** come from the Wikipedia infoboxes, which cite ESPNcricinfo. Kohli's are as of 4 Nov 2026. Smith's are a snapshot from 22 Oct 2026, which is still current for Tests because he missed the 27–31 Oct Cape Town Test.
- **Innings counts and strike rates** come from two undated profile aggregators.
- **Splits** rest on dated articles and search excerpts, and each split carries its own date. ESPNcricinfo profiles, Statsguru, HowSTAT and Cricbuzz all refused automated access during this research.

## Identity header

**Virat Kohli (India)**

- **Role:** right-hand top-order batter. He bowls right-arm medium but never took a Test wicket.
- **Born:** 5 Nov 1988, Delhi.
- **Debuts:** ODI 18 Aug 2008 v Sri Lanka; T20I 12 Jun 2010 v Zimbabwe; Test 20 Jun 2011 v West Indies.
- **Retirements:**
  - T20Is: after India won the 2024 T20 World Cup. His last T20I was the final, 29 Jun 2024 v South Africa, where he was Player of the Match.
  - Tests: announced 12 May 2025. His last Test was 3 Jan 2025 v Australia.
- **Status:** active in ODIs. He was named in India's squad for the five-match ODI series in New Zealand (4–15 Nov 2026) and has said the 2027 World Cup will be his last.
- **Captaincy:** Test captain from the 2014–15 Australia tour until he stepped down in 2022.
- **Ranking:** ICC ODI batting No. 3 (781 points, 3 Nov 2026), after regaining No. 1 in Jan 2026.
- **Name:** no ambiguity.

**Steven Peter Devereux Smith (Australia)**

- **Role:** right-hand top-order batter; he opened in Tests for a spell from Jan 2024. He bowls right-arm leg-break and came into the side as a leg-spinner before batting became his main job.
- **Born:** 2 Jun 1989, Kogarah, New South Wales.
- **Debuts:** T20I 5 Feb 2010 v Pakistan; ODI 19 Feb 2010 v West Indies; Test 13 Jul 2010 v Pakistan.
- **Retirements:** ODIs in Mar 2025, after the Champions Trophy semi-final loss to India (last ODI 4 Mar 2025). His last T20I was 25 Feb 2024 v New Zealand.
- **Status:** active in Tests, taking his future one series at a time.
  - Last Test: 18 Oct 2026 v South Africa at Gqeberha.
  - Injury: ruled out of the Cape Town Test with a cervical facet joint (neck) injury.
  - Target return: v New Zealand in Perth, 9 Dec 2026.
- **Captaincy:** Test captain until March 2018, when he stood down during the Cape Town ball-tampering Test. He has been stand-in captain several times since, including the 4–1 Ashes win of 2025–26.
- **Ranking:** ICC Test batting No. 5 (790 points on 3 Nov 2026; still fifth in the 11 Nov list).
- **Disambiguation:** this is Steven Smith born 1989. It is not Steven Barry Smith (Australia, three Tests in 1984) and not Graeme Smith of South Africa.

## Context that cuts both ways

- **Home conditions.** Kohli's 55.58 at home came mostly on Indian surfaces that favoured spin. Smith's 59.56 came on harder, bouncier Australian pitches. Both are strong home records. The difference is how they travelled: Kohli's average fell 14 runs away from home, Smith's about 5.
- **England.** Kohli's 33.21 in England came against the Anderson–Broad attack with a moving Dukes ball. That was the hardest assignment of the period for a batter who drives through cover on the up. His supporters can fairly argue his method was built to score, not to survive the outswinger. His critics can answer that Smith played in the same country, in the same era, and averaged 58.51 with eight hundreds.
- **Captaincy load.** Kohli captained India through his peak and is their most successful Test captain, so part of his Test value does not show in his batting average. Smith's leadership record is shorter and carries the 2018 sanction.
- **The missing year.** Smith's ban (Mar 2018 to Mar 2019) took a season out of his prime, and he came back with 774 runs in the 2019 Ashes. Without the ban, his aggregate would be higher still. Kohli lost no time to sanctions but lost about five years of form.
- **Era.** They played across the same 2010–2025 window against largely the same attacks, so era adjustments mostly cancel out. This is about as level a comparison as cricket offers.
- **An unfinished career.** Kohli's Test figures are final; Smith's are still moving. Smith is 37, has a neck injury, and is taking his future one series at a time. Even a poor final season would close only a little of the 8.4-run gap.
- **Format scope.** Across all formats Kohli's body of work is the larger: 28,601 international runs as of 4 Nov 2026, 86 international hundreds, and second on the all-time international run list after passing Sangakkara in Jan 2026. Smith's case rests on one format.

## Verdict

**Steve Smith is the better Test batter, by a clear margin. Virat Kohli is the better batter across formats, also by a clear margin.**

- **The Test numbers.** Smith averages 8.4 runs more (55.29 v 46.85) over almost the same number of matches. He also leads on:
  - hundreds: 37 v 30
  - the home-away drop: about 5 runs v 14
  - England: 58.51 v 33.21

  Kohli leads only on strike rate (55.57 v 53.83), highest score, and double hundreds (7 v 4).
- **Peak versus duration.** At their peaks they were close: Kohli was No. 1 and the ICC Test Cricketer of the Year in 2018, with double hundreds in four straight series. The difference is how long the peak lasted. Smith held his level for roughly a decade; Kohli averaged about 30 from 2020 to 2024.
- **ICC verdict.** The ICC named Smith its Test Cricketer of the Decade for 2011–2020. It named Kohli the overall and ODI Cricketer of the Decade. That split matches this one.
- **Peak rating.** Smith's 947 is the highest rating by anyone except Bradman, so Kohli's peak was necessarily lower.
- **Method.** The weakness that defined Kohli's late Test career, edging balls outside off stump, is exactly what Smith's trigger movement is built to neutralise.
- **Ratings.** The attribute table shows the same split: 66 each overall, but 27–21 to Smith on the three Test-specific rows.

**When the verdict would flip or narrow**

- **"Better batter", with no format named:** Kohli wins. His ODI record (59.07 with a record 55 hundreds), his T20I record (48.69 at a strike rate of 137) and his aggregate are far beyond Smith's white-ball numbers.
- **Only the 2016–2019 peak:** the case narrows sharply. Kohli's double hundreds in four straight series and his 2018 No. 1 ranking stand against Smith's two ICC Test awards and his 947 peak. I did not verify year-by-year averages for that window, so I would not call it either way.
- **Captaincy counted as part of the job:** Kohli's record as India's most successful Test captain, including the first series win in Australia, narrows the gap. It does not close it on batting alone.
- **Smith's away average after Feb 2025:** if it has fallen sharply (unconfirmed), the argument from conditions weakens. His figures in England and India would still favour him.
