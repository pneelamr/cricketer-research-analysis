# Virat Kohli vs Steve Smith: across formats, and who is the better Test batter

## Short answer

**Steve Smith is the better Test batter, by a clear margin.** He has more runs, a higher average, more hundreds, a better away record, a higher peak and a stronger late career. Kohli leads on double hundreds, on turning fifties into hundreds, and in South Africa. None of these comes close to closing an 8.4-run gap in career average or a gap of about 12 runs in away average.

**Across formats, Kohli is the better batter, by an even wider margin.** His ODI record is the best in the history of the format, and his T20I record is elite. Smith's ODI record is good rather than great, and his T20I record is modest.

Data is current to mid-November 2026. Kohli's Test record is final: he retired from Tests in May 2025. Smith's Test record runs to 22 October 2026, which takes in the second Test of the South Africa tour in Gqeberha. I could not confirm his scores in the third Test at Newlands (27-31 October), which Australia lost to go down 2-1 in the series.

## Test cricket in detail

### 1. Headline numbers

Smith has about 1,770 more runs than Kohli from four more Tests, at an average 8.4 higher and with seven more hundreds. Among batters with 10,000 or more Test runs, only Kumar Sangakkara (57.40) averages more than Smith. Kohli's 46.85 is lower than the averages of the other two "Fab Four" batters, Joe Root and Kane Williamson.

**Where Kohli leads:**
- **Double hundreds:** He has 7, an Indian record, against Smith's 4. Six of Kohli's came at home and one in Antigua.
- **Conversion:** Kohli turned 30 of his 61 fifty-plus scores into hundreds (49%), against Smith's 37 of 82 (45%).
- **The catch:** Smith reaches fifty much more often, in about 36% of his innings against Kohli's 29%. So Kohli's better conversion rate still leaves him with fewer hundreds.

### 2. Home and away

This is where the gap opens.

| | Home | Away |
|---|---|---|
| Smith | 62 Tests, 5,301 runs at 59.56 (Aug 2026 report) | 56 Tests, 5,006 runs at 53.82 (Oct 2026 report) |
| Kohli | 55 Tests, 4,336 runs at 55.59, 14 hundreds | 68 Tests, 4,894 runs at ~41.1, 16 hundreds (includes two neutral-venue WTC finals) |

Smith's two splits come from separate news reports and leave out neutral venues, so they do not add up to his career total. I built Kohli's split from a published 99-Test snapshot plus his later home scores. It matches his career totals exactly, but Statsguru was not reachable to check it.

Smith's away average (53.82) is higher than Kohli's career average (46.85). Kohli dropped from about 56 at home to about 41 away; Smith dropped from about 60 to about 54.

The fairest test is each batter in the other's hardest conditions. Kohli averaged 33 in England and 47 in Australia. Smith averaged 50 in India and about 48 across Asia. Smith's floor abroad is higher, and his away record is more consistent.

### 3. Peak and decline

**Smith's peak was higher and lasted longer:**
- He averaged 81.85, 73.70, 71.93 and 76.76 in the calendar years 2014 to 2017.
- After his year-long ban, he scored 774 runs at 110.57 in four Tests in the 2019 Ashes.
- His peak ICC rating was 947 (December 2017). Only Bradman's 961 is higher.
- He was the No.1-ranked Test batter for long stretches between 2015 and 2020.

**Kohli's peak was elite, but a level below Smith's:**
- 2016-19: 43 Tests, 4,208 runs at 66.79, 16 hundreds. His yearly averages were 75.93, 75.64, 55.08 and 68.00.
- In 2018 he scored 1,322 runs, most of them in South Africa, England and Australia. It is one of the great years by a touring batter.
- His peak ICC rating was 937 (August 2018, after 97 and 103 at Trent Bridge), 11th-highest of all time. He was No.1 for much of 2018-19.

**The decline decides it:**
- **Kohli, 2020 to his last Test (January 2025):** 39 Tests, 2,028 runs at 30.72, 3 hundreds (186 at Ahmedabad, 121 at Port of Spain, 100* at Perth). His yearly averages were 19.3 (2020), 28.2 (2021), 26.5 (2022), 55.9 (2023), 24.5 (2024), plus 23 runs in his one Test of 2025. An ESPNcricinfo analysis put him near the bottom of established top-order batters for 2020-24. He retired four months after the 2024-25 Australia series.
- **Smith had his own slump:** between his Lord's hundred in June 2023 and his Gabba hundred in December 2024, he went 12 Tests without a century. That run included a failed four-Test spell as an opener (171 runs at 28.50).
- **The difference is that Smith recovered:**
  - 101 at the Gabba and 140 at the MCG against India
  - 141 and 131 at Galle
  - an unbeaten 129 at the SCG in January 2026 for his 37th hundred
  - 11,000 Test runs in October 2026, at 37

### 4. Batting as captain

Both batted superbly as captain, which undercuts any argument that the captaincy explains the gap between them.
- **Kohli:** 68 Tests as captain (40 won, 17 lost, 11 drawn), 5,864 runs at 54.80 with 20 hundreds.
- **Smith:** 44 Tests as captain (26 won, 11 lost, 7 drawn), averaging close to 70 with 18 hundreds.

As a leader, Kohli's record is the stronger one: India reached No.1 in the Test rankings and won a series in Australia for the first time. That is a separate question from batting.

### 5. Match impact

- **WTC finals:** Smith made 121 in the 2023 final, which Australia won. Kohli made 44 and 13 in the 2021 final and 14 and 49 in the 2023 final, both lost by India.
- **Kohli's best away hundreds came in defeats:** 141 at Adelaide (2014), 149 at Edgbaston (2018), 153 at Centurion (2018) and 123 at Perth (2018). That reflects India's bowling in those matches as much as his batting, but it is a real gap in the record.
- **Away wins:** an older published comparison found Smith averaging 98.90 in away Test wins against Kohli's 41.88. That figure predates the last several years of both careers.

### 6. Awards

- **Smith:** ICC Test Player of the Decade (2011-20), Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy 2015, ICC Test Player of the Year 2015 and 2017.
- **Kohli:** Sobers Trophy 2017 and 2018, ICC Test Player of the Year 2018, ICC Male Cricketer of the Decade, ICC ODI Player of the Decade.

The two decade awards sum up the consensus: Smith was the Test batter of the decade, and Kohli was the white-ball and all-round player of the decade.

### 7. The strongest case for Kohli

- His 2016-19 peak, at 66.79 over 43 Tests, compares with almost anyone's in the modern era.
- He was better in South Africa than Smith has been.
- He hit seven double hundreds and was a leading reason India's away results improved.
- He did all of this while captaining a side through its hardest tours.

That case does not survive the career totals, the away splits, or the five-year average of 30.72 he carried into retirement while Smith was still averaging 55 at 37.

### Test verdict: Smith

Smith has the better overall record, the better record away from home and in his opponents' conditions, the higher peak, and the stronger finish. Kohli's Test career was very good, with an outstanding 2016-19, but over the full career it sits a tier below Smith's.

## ODIs: Kohli, decisively

- **Kohli:** 318 ODIs, 15,183 runs at 59.07, 55 hundreds, an all-time record (Tendulkar made 49).
  - He is second on the all-time run list, behind Tendulkar.
  - 25 of his hundreds came in successful chases.
  - He won the 2011 World Cup and the 2025 Champions Trophy, and was Player of the Tournament at the 2023 World Cup with a record 765 runs.
  - He is still playing, with the 2027 World Cup in view.
- **Smith:** 170 ODIs, 5,800 runs at 43.28, strike rate 86.96, 12 hundreds, highest 164.
  - He retired in March 2025 after the Champions Trophy semi-final.
  - He won two World Cups, scoring 105 against India in the 2015 semi-final.
  - He was a good top-order ODI batter, not a defining one.

## T20Is: Kohli, decisively

- **Kohli:** 125 T20Is, 4,188 runs at 48.69, strike rate 137.04, one hundred and 38 fifties.
  - He was Player of the Tournament at the 2014 and 2016 World Cups.
  - He was Player of the Match in the 2024 final with 76, then retired from the format.
- **Smith:** 67 T20Is, 1,094 runs at 24.86, strike rate 125.46.
  - He was in the 2021 World Cup-winning squad.
  - He was left out of the original 2026 World Cup squad and added later as Josh Hazlewood's replacement after a strong BBL. He was not picked for at least one match as Australia went out in the group stage.

## Overall

International totals: Kohli has about 28,600 runs and 86 hundreds; only Tendulkar has more international hundreds. Smith has about 17,900 runs and 49 hundreds. Tests carry the most weight in historical rankings, and that is where Smith is clearly ahead. Kohli's ODI record alone still makes him the more complete batter of the two. **For Tests, pick Smith. Across all formats, pick Kohli.**

## How reliable these numbers are

- **Taken from a cited source:** career totals, Kohli's captaincy figures, Smith's home and away splits, both peak ratings, Kohli's records in Australia and South Africa, and Smith's records in India and the Ashes.
- **Worked out by me from match scores, with Statsguru unreachable:** Kohli's home/away split and his England, New Zealand and SENA totals. Each is internally consistent with his career totals.
- **From my own knowledge, not re-checked in this session:** the year-by-year averages for Smith (2014-17) and Kohli (2016-19).
- **Still open:** Smith's third-Test scores at Newlands. Those could move his average by a few tenths, but not the verdict.
