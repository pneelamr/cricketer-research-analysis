# Sachin Tendulkar — Research Dossier

Taking this as **Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar of India** (retired 2013). The only cricketing namesake of note is his son Arjun Tendulkar, a left-arm seamer in Indian domestic and IPL cricket, who is ruled out here.

## Identity header

- **Full name:** Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar (ESPNcricinfo short form "SR Tendulkar"; widely known as the "Little Master" and "Master Blaster")
- **Born:** 24 April 1973, Bombay (now Mumbai), Maharashtra, India
- **Country / teams:** India (1989–2013); Bombay/Mumbai (1988–2013); Yorkshire (1992, the county's first overseas player); East Bengal (1994, per Wikipedia infobox); Mumbai Indians, IPL (2008–2013)
- **Role:** Right-hand top-order batter — Test No. 4 for most of his career, ODI opener from 1994; occasional bowler
- **Batting / bowling style:** Right-hand bat; right-arm leg-break and off-break (as the record books list him), and he also bowled medium pace
- **International debuts:** Test — 15 November 1989 v Pakistan, Karachi (cap 187, aged 16 years 205 days) · ODI — 18 December 1989 v Pakistan · T20I — 1 December 2006 v South Africa (his only T20I)
- **Status:** Retired. Last ODI 18 March 2012 v Pakistan (ODI retirement announced 23 December 2012); final T20 cricket at the 2013 Champions League T20; 200th and final Test 14–16 November 2013 v West Indies at the Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai. Captained India in two spells (1996–97 and 1999–2000): 25 Tests and 73 ODIs.
- **Stats as of:** Final career figures (career ended 16 November 2013). Headline numbers taken from the ESPNcricinfo-sourced tables reproduced on Wikipedia (infobox dated 15 November 2013) and cricketweb.net StatsSpider, cross-checked against HowSTAT and ESPNcricinfo search-result snippets. Retrieved 15–16 November 2026.

## Career arc

- **Prodigy (1988–89).** Ranji Trophy debut for Bombay v Gujarat at the Wankhede on 11 December 1988, aged 15: 100* from 129 balls, the youngest Indian to score a first-class hundred on debut. Secondary sources also credit him with hundreds on debut in the Duleep and Irani Trophies.
- **Teenage Test debut (1989–90).** Test debut at Karachi on 15 November 1989 at 16 years 205 days (scored 15) against an attack of Imran Khan, Wasim Akram and Waqar Younis; ODI debut the following month. First Test hundred — 119* at Old Trafford, 9 August 1990, aged 17 — saved the match.
- **Yorkshire and the overseas hundreds (1991–93).** Became Yorkshire's first overseas player in 1992 at 19. By his early 20s he had Test hundreds in Australia, England and South Africa; across the career he made Test hundreds against every Test-playing nation, at 30 grounds, 27 of them outside India.
- **Reinvention as an ODI opener (1994).** Promoted to open in ODIs against New Zealand in March 1994; first ODI hundred (110 v Australia, Colombo) came on 9 September 1994, in his 79th ODI. From there he became the defining ODI batter of the 1990s: 49 ODI hundreds, 30 of them away or at neutral venues, seven at Sharjah alone.
- **Captaincy (1996–97 and 1999–2000).** Two unhappy spells. The first ended in early 1998 after poor results; the second, succeeding Azharuddin, included a 0–3 Test series loss in Australia and ended when he resigned after a 0–2 home defeat to South Africa in 2000, with Sourav Ganguly taking over. Overall record: Tests 4–9–12 from 25; ODIs 23 wins from 73.
- **Peak against Australia (1998).** 155*, 79 and 177 in consecutive Test innings against Shane Warne's Australia at home, then the Sharjah "Desert Storm" — 143 off 131 balls in 41°C heat on 22 April 1998 (India lost but qualified for the final), followed by 134 in the final two days later, on his 25th birthday. Wisden later voted the 143 the ODI innings of the 1990s.
- **2003 World Cup.** 673 runs in the tournament (then the record for a single edition) and Player of the Tournament as India reached the final.
- **Double hundreds and the injury years (2004–07).** 241* at Sydney (January 2004), reportedly made while deliberately abstaining from the cover drive after a run of dismissals, then his highest score, 248* v Bangladesh at Dhaka (December 2004). A tennis-elbow injury diagnosed in 2004 kept him from lifting a bat for about four and a half months; a shoulder operation followed in 2006, a groin strain in 2008, and a 2001 right-foot injury flared again in 2011. His form dipped noticeably in 2005–07.
- **Late renaissance (2008–11).** Passed Brian Lara as Test cricket's leading run-scorer in October 2008; scored the first double century in men's ODIs (200* v South Africa, Gwalior, 24 February 2010) at 36; won the ICC's Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy as Cricketer of the Year 2010 at 37; won the 2010 IPL Orange Cap (618 runs, then a single-season record) as Mumbai Indians captain; and was part of the 2011 World Cup-winning side in his sixth World Cup, scoring 482 runs at 53.55 with two hundreds (second-highest aggregate behind Tillakaratne Dilshan).
- **The 100th hundred (2012).** After roughly a year stuck on 99, his 100th international century — 114 v Bangladesh at Mirpur, 16 March 2012 — made him the first (and still only) player to reach the mark.
- **Farewell (2012–13).** Retired from ODIs on 23 December 2012; won the IPL with Mumbai Indians in May 2013 and retired from T20 cricket after the 2013 Champions League; announced Test retirement on 10 October 2013 and played his 200th Test at the Wankhede on 14–16 November 2013, scoring 74 in his last innings. The Bharat Ratna was announced for him the day the match ended.

## Playing style and technical profile

- **Setup and method.** A compact, orthodox, side-on technique built on a still head and early judgement of length; equally comfortable off front and back foot. He has said the backlift is the most important factor in adapting across formats (remarks reported in 2026).
- **Signature shots.** The straight drive, described on Wikipedia as "picture-perfect" and the shot most associated with him; a wristy leg-side flick; a back-foot punch through the off side; and the paddle sweep and scoop over short fine leg, both of which he is credited with popularising. The upper cut over the slips against genuine pace became a trademark of his later ODI years.
- **Against pace.** Proven from the first tour: a teenager against Imran, Wasim and Waqar in 1989, then hundreds against Australia (20 international hundreds against them — 11 in Tests, 9 in ODIs), South Africa and Pakistan across two decades. His 51 Test hundreds came against every opponent and mostly away from home.
- **Against spin.** Used the sweep and paddle as much as his feet. The 1998 home series against Warne (155* at Chennai, 177 at Bangalore) is the reference point; his preparation for it, reportedly facing leg-spin into roughs created outside leg stump in the nets, is part of the folklore.
- **ODI batting.** As an opener he combined a career strike rate of 86.23 across an era when that was fast with an average of 44.83 — and the two numbers held together over 452 innings. Sharjah (seven ODI hundreds) and tri-series finals were where the attacking side of his game showed most.
- **Adaptability and self-denial.** The 241* at Sydney and the 200* at 36 illustrate the two poles: he could remove a whole shot from his game for a day, and he could still set a new ceiling for the format in his late 30s.
- **Bowling.** Bowled all three of leg-spin, off-spin and medium pace, usually as a partnership-breaker; 154 ODI wickets at 44.48 with a best of 5/32 is a genuine fifth-bowler contribution over a career, and 46 Test wickets. His ODI economy of 5.10 is respectable for a part-timer.
- **Fielding.** A safe outfielder in his youth and a reliable catcher later (255 international catches); never regarded as an elite fielder.
- **Temperament and the "finisher" debate.** Undemonstrative and rarely sanctioned; the recurring criticism was that his hundreds too often came in defeats and that he was not a fourth-innings finisher. The record is mixed rather than damning: India won 20 of the Tests and 33 of the ODIs in which he scored a hundred, and he averaged 56.95 across 45 World Cup matches.

## After playing

- **Parliament:** Nominated member of the Rajya Sabha, 27 April 2012 to 26 April 2018 (sworn in 4 June 2012), overlapping his playing career; his attendance (about 8%) drew criticism.
- **Philanthropy:** UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador since November 2013; sponsored the education of 200 children through the Mumbai NGO Apnalaya from 2009; runs the Sachin Tendulkar Foundation; in April 2026 was reported among those raising funds for the ailing former team-mate Vinod Kambli.
- **Books and film:** Autobiography *Playing It My Way* (6 November 2014); docudrama *Sachin: A Billion Dreams* (2017).
- **Playing again:** Captained India Masters to the inaugural International Masters League title (February–March 2025, beating Brian Lara's West Indies Masters by six wickets in the Raipur final). Season 2, scheduled for 24 October–14 November 2026, was reported postponed.
- **Trophy in his name:** The India–England Test series became the Anderson–Tendulkar Trophy from June 2025, replacing the Pataudi and Anthony de Mello trophies; the renaming drew criticism, and Tendulkar himself asked the boards to retain the Pataudi legacy (the Pataudi Medal now goes to the winning captain).
- **Family:** His son Arjun Tendulkar plays IPL cricket (Mumbai Indians, then reported with Lucknow Super Giants in 2026).
- Brand endorsements and media appearances continue; no formal coaching or commentary role is documented in the sources used here.

## Off-field and context

- **Captaincy record:** Two spells, both ending in resignation or removal; Tests 4–9–12, ODIs 23 wins from 73 (sources above).
- **2001 Mike Denness affair:** During the Port Elizabeth Test in November 2001, match referee Mike Denness handed him a suspended one-match ban for alleged ball tampering after TV pictures showed him cleaning the seam without informing the umpires. The sanction caused a diplomatic row between the BCCI and ICC.
- **2008 "Monkeygate":** As a witness in the Harbhajan Singh–Andrew Symonds hearing he initially said he could not say exactly what Harbhajan had said, later stating Harbhajan had used a Hindi expletive rather than a racial slur; the handling of the hearing remains disputed in Australian and Indian media.
- **Injuries:** Tennis elbow (2004, out about four and a half months), shoulder surgery (2006), groin strain (2008), a 2001 foot injury that recurred in 2011.
- **Gifts and politics:** Received a Ferrari 360 Modena from Fiat, presented by Michael Schumacher, after his 29th Test hundred (2002); the later import-duty controversy is not covered in the sources used here and is left unverified. Rajya Sabha attendance was documented as low (see above).
