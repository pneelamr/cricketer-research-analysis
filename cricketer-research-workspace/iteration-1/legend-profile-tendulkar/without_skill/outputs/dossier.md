As of November 2026 the Test run record is under active threat from Joe Root; the ODI century record (49) was passed by Virat Kohli in 2023.

## 2. Early life and the making of a prodigy

- Born at Nirmal Nursing Home, Dadar, into a Marathi family. Father Ramesh Tendulkar was a Marathi novelist, poet and professor; mother Rajni worked in insurance. He was named after composer Sachin Dev Burman. Two half-brothers (Nitin, Ajit) and a half-sister (Savita).
- As a child he was drawn to tennis and idolised John McEnroe. His elder brother Ajit introduced him to cricket in 1984 and to coach Ramakant Achrekar at Shivaji Park. Achrekar moved him to Sharadashram Vidyamandir school for its cricket programme.
- Achrekar's famous drill: a one-rupee coin placed on the stumps went to any bowler who dismissed Tendulkar; if he survived the session, he kept the coin. He has said the 13 coins he won are among his most prized possessions.
- 1987: trained at the MRF Pace Foundation as an aspiring fast bowler; Dennis Lillee advised him to concentrate on batting.
- 1988: with Vinod Kambli, an unbroken 664-run partnership in a Harris Shield school match (Tendulkar 326*), which brought national attention. In the 1987/88 season he scored over 1,000 runs in the Under-16 tournament.
- 11 December 1988: first-class debut for Bombay v Gujarat at the Wankhede, aged 15, scoring 100* — the youngest Indian to make a century on first-class debut. He went on to score centuries on debut in the Deodhar and Duleep Trophies as well.

## 3. International career: a phased timeline

### Phase 1: Teenage debut (1989–1993)
- Test debut 15 November 1989 v Pakistan, Karachi, aged 16 years 205 days, facing Imran Khan, Wasim Akram and Waqar Younis. Famously took a Waqar bouncer on the nose in the Sialkot Test and batted on.
- ODI debut 18 December 1989 v Pakistan, Gujranwala (out for 0).
- First Test century: 119* at Old Trafford, 14 August 1990, aged 17, saving the match.
- 1991–92 Australia tour: 148* at Sydney and 114 at Perth, aged 18, on a fast bouncy WACA pitch. These innings established him as a world-class batter.
- 1992: first overseas-born player for Yorkshire (16 matches, 1,070 runs at 46.52).

### Phase 2: ODI opener and global superstar (1994–1999)
- Promoted to open in ODIs in Auckland, March 1994 (82 off 49 balls). The move transformed his ODI output.
- First ODI century: 110 v Australia, Colombo, 9 September 1994, in his 79th ODI.
- 1996 World Cup: tournament top scorer with 523 runs.
- Captain for the first time in 1996 (see Captaincy).
- 1998: arguably his peak year. 1,894 ODI runs in a calendar year (still the record) with 9 ODI centuries; 12 international centuries in all. The "Desert Storm" innings in Sharjah: 143 v Australia on 22 April 1998 (through a sandstorm, to qualify for the final) and 134 in the final on 24 April, his 25th birthday. Also 155* at Chennai v Australia in the Tests that spring, and a notable duel with Shane Warne.
- 1999: 136 at Chennai v Pakistan in a losing cause (India lost by 12 runs), batting through back pain. During the 1999 World Cup his father died; he flew home for the funeral, returned and scored 140* v Kenya.

### Phase 3: Elite consistency, injuries, and the Bradman chase (2000–2007)
- 2002: 117 at Port of Spain equalled Bradman's 29 Test centuries; 193 at Headingley in August 2002 was his 30th. Wisden's 2002 all-time ratings placed him second among Test batters (behind Bradman) and second among ODI batters (behind Viv Richards).
- 2003 World Cup: 673 runs in 11 matches (a single-tournament record at the time), including 98 v Pakistan at Centurion; Player of the Tournament as India reached the final.
- January 2004: 241* at Sydney, famously abstaining from the cover drive for the whole innings after a run of dismissals in that region.
- 2004–05: tennis-elbow injury kept him out of batting for roughly four and a half months; critics began questioning his place. December 2004: 248* v Bangladesh at Dhaka, his highest Test score.
- 10 December 2005: 35th Test century at Delhi v Sri Lanka, breaking Sunil Gavaskar's record.
- 2006: shoulder surgery; booed at the Wankhede after a low score v England. 2007 World Cup: group-stage exit, calls for his retirement, and public tension with coach Greg Chappell.
- 1 December 2006: his only T20I, India's first-ever, at Johannesburg. Scored 10 off 12 balls (bowled by Charl Langeveldt) and took one wicket for 12 (Justin Kemp, per two sources).

### Phase 4: The second peak (2008–2011)
- January 2008: 154* at the SCG. October 2008: passed Brian Lara to become the leading Test run-scorer.
- December 2008: 103* at Chennai chasing 387 v England, days after the Mumbai attacks, dedicated to the city.
- November 2009: 175 v Australia at Hyderabad chasing 351 (lost by 3 runs).
- 24 February 2010: 200* v South Africa at Gwalior, the first double century in men's ODI history.
- 2010: Sir Garfield Sobers Trophy (ICC Cricketer of the Year); Wisden Leading Cricketer in the World; Time 100; named Honorary Group Captain in the Indian Air Force. Reached 14,000 Test runs in October 2010.
- 2011 World Cup: 482 runs at 53.55 with centuries v England (120, Bengaluru) and South Africa (111, Nagpur), 85 in the semi-final v Pakistan at Mohali, and 18 in the final. India beat Sri Lanka at the Wankhede on 2 April 2011, his sixth World Cup and first title. Teammates carried him on their shoulders around the ground; Virat Kohli said Tendulkar "has carried the burden of the nation for 21 years, it was time we carried him."

### Phase 5: The hundredth hundred and farewell (2011–2013)
- The wait for the 100th international century lasted a year and 33 innings and became a national obsession. It came on 16 March 2012: 114 v Bangladesh at Mirpur in the Asia Cup (India lost the match).
- Last ODI: 18 March 2012 v Pakistan, Mirpur. Announced ODI retirement on 23 December 2012.
- 10 October 2013: announced he would retire after the two-Test home series v West Indies, which the BCCI scheduled in Kolkata and Mumbai so his 200th Test would be at the Wankhede.
- 14–16 November 2013: 200th Test. Scored 74 in his final innings (finishing 79 runs short of 16,000 Test runs). India won by an innings and 126 runs inside three days. His unscripted farewell speech on 16 November, thanking his family, coach, teammates and the crowd, is among the most-watched moments in Indian sport. The government announced the Bharat Ratna the same evening.

## 5. Records and milestones (selected)

Records he still holds (as of November 2026):
- Most Test runs (15,921), most Test centuries (51), most Tests (200).
- Most ODI runs (18,426), most ODIs (463), most ODI runs in a calendar year (1,894 in 1998), most ODI centuries in a calendar year (9 in 1998).
- Most international runs (34,357), most international centuries (100), most international matches (664).
- Most World Cup runs (2,278) and centuries (6).
- Most ODI Player of the Match awards (62) and Player of the Series awards (15).
- First double century in men's ODIs; first to 15,000 and 16,000 ODI runs; first to 50,000 runs in all recognised cricket (5 October 2013).
- Only player to have scored a century on debut in all three premier Indian domestic competitions (Ranji, Duleep, Deodhar).

Records that have since fallen:
- Most ODI centuries (49): Virat Kohli equalled it at the 2023 World Cup and passed it at the Wankhede on 15 November 2023 with Tendulkar in the stands. Kohli stood at 55 ODI centuries and 86 international centuries as of late September 2026.
- Most Test fifties (68): passed by Joe Root in 2026 (69 at last report).
- Most fourth-innings Test runs: passed by Root in 2024.
- Most runs in a single World Cup (673 in 2003): passed by Kohli (765) at the 2023 World Cup.

Two spells: 1996–97 (took over from Mohammad Azharuddin) and 1999–2000 (0–3 in Australia, then 0–2 at home to South Africa, after which he resigned and Sourav Ganguly took over). He regarded captaincy as a burden that hurt his batting and declined it later; in 2007, when offered the job by BCCI president Sharad Pawar, he recommended M.S. Dhoni instead.

## 7. Batting style and technique

- Cross-dominant: bats and bowls right-handed, writes left-handed.
- A compact, balanced technique built on a still head and early judgment of length. Trademark strokes: the straight drive, the back-foot punch through cover and point, the paddle sweep, the upper cut (used to great effect against Shoaib Akhtar at Centurion in 2003), and the wristy leg-side flick.
- Bradman reportedly told his wife that Tendulkar's technique and compactness reminded him of his own; Gavaskar praised his blend of classical technique and aggression.
- Analysts (including John Buchanan) identified early-innings vulnerability to the short ball and to left-arm pace; after the 2004–06 injuries his game became more risk-averse and accumulative, especially in Tests.
- Useful part-time bowler who could bowl seam, off-spin and leg-spin; 201 international wickets, including 5/32 v Australia at Kochi (1998) and the famous final over to seal the 1993 Hero Cup semi-final v South Africa.

## 9. Post-retirement life

### Public roles
- **Rajya Sabha (2012–2018):** nominated by President Pratibha Patil while still an active player. His attendance (around 8%) drew sustained criticism; he asked 22 questions and took part in no debates. He donated his full salary and allowances to the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund.
- **ICC Global Ambassador** for the 2023 Men's World Cup; carried the trophy out at the Ahmedabad opener.
- **Masters cricket comeback:** captained India Masters to the inaugural International Masters League title in March 2025 (beat Brian Lara's West Indies Masters by six wickets in Raipur on 16 March). Aged 51, he scored 181 runs in six innings at a strike rate of 153, with a best of 64 off 33 balls v Australia Masters. He launched the league alongside Sunil Gavaskar in September 2024.

### Business and brand
- Early deals set Indian sports-marketing benchmarks: WorldTel (1995, ₹30 crore over five years; renewed 2001 for ₹80 crore); Saatchi & Saatchi marketing rights (2006, ₹180 crore over three years). Forbes ranked him 51st among the world's highest-paid athletes in 2013 (US$22m).
- Sports ownership: co-owner of Kerala Blasters FC (ISL) 2014–2017; Bengaluru Blasters (badminton, 2016); reported co-owner of Tamil Thalaivas (Pro Kabaddi); joined the ownership group of the USA's National Cricket League in October 2024 and rang the NYSE opening bell for it in October 2025; invested in the Indian Street Premier League (January 2024).
- Equity investments: Spinny, Unacademy, Sugar.fit, Kissht, RRP Electronics, Azad Engineering, SRT10 Athleisure, Truzon Solar (₹3.6 crore for a reported 2% stake, December 2025), and Recykal (strategic investor, announced 13 November 2026, extending the waste-management platform's US$23m bridge round).
- Own brands: the sportswear label TEN x YOU (launched October 2025); the SRT10 academy ecosystem (flagship SRT10 Global Academy at DY Patil, Navi Mumbai; SRT10 Altevol Centre of Excellence opened in Ahmedabad in April 2026); the Tendulkar Middlesex Global Academy (2018 tie-up with Middlesex CCC); co-owns the restaurants Tendulkar's and Sachin's in Mumbai.
- Endorsements (2024–2026): Bank of Baroda (global ambassador), Apollo Tyres, Ageas Federal Life, Luminous, Tanishq, Gillette, Hafele, Chitale Bandhu, Organic India, Reddit (June 2025), SML (July 2026).
- Valuation: Kroll's India Celebrity Brand Valuation 2025 ranks him fourth at US$125.9m, up 12.2%, the only retired athlete consistently in the top ten. Outlook Business (April 2026) cites unnamed reports putting his net worth at roughly US$170m, with his Bandra West home valued at ₹100–150 crore. Treat the net-worth figure as an estimate.

### Philanthropy: the Sachin Tendulkar Foundation
- Established around 2019–2020 (its five-year anniversary was marked in January 2025). Directors include his wife Anjali and, since December 2024, daughter Sara.
- Gates Foundation partnership announced 25 March 2025, followed in April by an MoU with the Meghalaya government on child immunisation, nutrition and healthcare.
- Dantewada, Chhattisgarh: 50 village playgrounds and the "Maidan Cup" with the Mann Deshi Foundation in a formerly Maoist-affected district (announced June 2025; Tendulkar visited and played with local children in April 2026).
- Professor Ramesh Tendulkar Memorial Fellowship (announced Teachers' Day, 5 September 2026): funds one MD doctor a year for specialised cleft and craniofacial training in Srinagar with the Inga Health Foundation, in memory of his father.
- Other work: facial-deformity surgeries in Jammu and Kashmir, rural-school sports facilities (Satara, Chittoor), free coaching for municipal-school children in Navi Mumbai, a clean-energy project in Guhir village with Schneider Electric (April 2026), and COVID-19 relief donations in 2020–21 (he was himself hospitalised with COVID in April 2021).

### Media
- Autobiography *Playing It My Way* (2014, with Boria Majumdar) set a Limca record for pre-orders (150,289).
- *Sachin: A Billion Dreams* (2017), a docudrama directed by James Erskine.
- 14 December 2025: hosted Lionel Messi at the Wankhede during Messi's India tour and gave him his signed No. 10 jersey from the 2011 World Cup; Messi reciprocated with a signed football.

## 10. Family

- Married Dr Anjali Mehta, a paediatrician, on 24 May 1995. They met at Mumbai airport in 1990.
- Daughter Sara (born 1997) is a director of the foundation and a public figure in her own right.
- Son Arjun (born 1999) is a left-arm seamer and lower-order batter. He moved from Mumbai to Goa in domestic cricket (century on Goa debut v Rajasthan, December 2022) and has passed 50 first-class wickets. He played five IPL matches for Mumbai Indians (3 wickets), was traded to Lucknow Super Giants for IPL 2026 (reported at ₹30 lakh; some reports describe a swap involving Shardul Thakur), and made his LSG debut against Punjab Kings on 23 May 2026. In August 2026 he confirmed he would continue with Goa.
- A Hindu and Ganesha devotee; was a follower of Sathya Sai Baba and cancelled his 2011 birthday celebrations on Sai Baba's death.

## 11. Controversies and criticisms

- **2001, Port Elizabeth:** match referee Mike Denness handed him a suspended one-match ban for alleged ball tampering (cleaning the seam without the umpire's permission). The charge caused a diplomatic row between the BCCI and ICC; the ICC later found him guilty only of failing to inform the umpire.
- **2002–03, the Ferrari:** Fiat gifted him a Ferrari 360 Modena (presented by Michael Schumacher) for equalling Bradman's 29 Test centuries. A government waiver of the import duty was challenged in court and became a political issue.
- **2008, "Monkeygate":** his evidence in the Harbhajan Singh–Andrew Symonds racism hearing changed between the first hearing and the appeal, which Australian players and media criticised.
- **Rajya Sabha attendance** (roughly 8% over six years).
- **2021, Pandora Papers:** named among those with offshore holdings; his representatives said the investments were legitimate and fully taxed.
- Perennial debate: whether he was a "match-winner," pointing to the number of his centuries in losing causes (notably in ODIs) and his captaincy record. The 2011 World Cup win and India's rise to the No. 1 Test ranking in 2009–2011 are the usual rebuttals.

## 12. Records under threat: where things stand in November 2026

**Joe Root and the Test run record.** Root has 14,278 runs from 169 Tests (as of early November 2026), 1,643 short of 15,921. He passed 14,000 in June 2026 and already holds the Test fifties and fourth-innings-runs records that were Tendulkar's. England tour South Africa from December 2026 to January 2027 and have a full 2027 home summer; their next Test tour of India is reportedly in early 2028, so the record could fall either in 2027 or, fittingly, in India in 2028, depending on form and fitness. Tendulkar, asked about it in a Reddit AMA, said it was "evident" early that Root would be a great and that records are meant to be broken.

**Virat Kohli and the century records.** Kohli holds the ODI century record (55) and is at 86 international centuries, 14 short of 100. At 37, and playing only ODIs, whether he reaches 100 depends on how long he continues after the 2027 World Cup.

**Records that look safe:** 200 Tests (Root is closest), 463 ODIs, 18,426 ODI runs (Kohli is roughly 4,000 short and plays one format), 1,894 ODI runs in a calendar year, and 100 international centuries in the near term.

## 13. Assessment

Tendulkar's claim to being the greatest batter of the modern era rests less on any single peak than on the combination of precocity (a Test century at 17, two in Australia at 18), longevity (24 years at the top, through two generations of bowlers, from Imran and Marshall to Steyn and Anderson), and volume (both all-time run records, 100 hundreds). His ODI record is arguably the stronger one: he effectively invented the modern aggressive opener's role in 1994 and his 1998 season remains unmatched. His Test average of 53.78 sits below Bradman and a handful of contemporaries (Sangakkara, Kallis, Steve Smith), which is the standard argument against ranking him first on pure quality.

The cultural dimension is inseparable from the cricket. He debuted two years before India's 1991 economic liberalisation and became the face of its satellite-television and consumer boom; the "Sachin, Sachin" chant, the nation's reaction to his 100th hundred, and the Bharat Ratna being extended to sport for him are markers of that. Thirteen years after his last innings he remains a top-five Indian celebrity brand, which has no parallel among retired athletes in India.

The post-retirement record is more mixed than the playing one: a near-invisible Rajya Sabha term and the Pandora Papers sit alongside a foundation that has grown into substantive partnerships (Gates Foundation, state governments) and a steady run of grassroots sports and child-health projects. The most recent chapter, 2025–2026, shows him active on all fronts at 53: captaining a title-winning masters side, opening academies, making clean-tech investments, and launching a medical fellowship in his father's name.
