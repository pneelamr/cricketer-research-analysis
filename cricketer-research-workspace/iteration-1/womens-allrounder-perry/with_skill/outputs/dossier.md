# Ellyse Perry — research dossier

Taking this as Ellyse Perry, the Australian all-rounder. There is no other prominent cricketer of this name, so no disambiguation was needed. She is active. Statistics below are stamped 15 November 2026; WBBL|12 is in progress, so her WBBL figures will move within days.

## Identity header

- **Full name:** Ellyse Alexandra Perry (known as "Pez")
- **Born:** 3 November 1990, Wahroonga, Sydney, New South Wales (age 36)
- **Country / teams:** Australia (2007–); New South Wales (2007-08 to 2018-19, and again from 2025-26) and Victoria (2019-20 to 2023-24) in domestic cricket; Sydney Sixers (WBBL, every season since 2015-16, contracted through WBBL|13); Royal Challengers Bengaluru (WPL, 2023–); Birmingham Phoenix (The Hundred, 2022–); shorter stints with Loughborough Lightning (2016–17), Supernovas (2018), Wellington (2024-25) and Hampshire (2025)
- **Role:** Batting all-rounder — top-order right-hand batter (No. 3/4 in Tests and ODIs, top three in T20 cricket) and right-arm fast-medium bowler, now used sparingly with the ball
- **Batting / bowling style:** Right-hand bat; right-arm fast-medium (cricket.com.au lists "right-arm pace")
- **International debuts:** ODI 22 July 2007 v New Zealand, aged 16 (then the youngest Australian to play senior international cricket); T20I 1 February 2008 v England; Test 15 February 2008 v England
- **Status:** Active in all three formats. Cricket Australia central contract for 2026-27. Playing WBBL|12 for the Sydney Sixers (season runs 29 Oct–5 Dec 2026). Never Australia's permanent captain; captained the Sydney Sixers from the inaugural WBBL season until handing over to Ashleigh Gardner on 7 November 2025.
- **Stats as of:** 15 November 2026, cricket.com.au player profile (international and WBBL tables), cross-checked against ESPNcricinfo figures dated 10 March 2026 via the Wikipedia infobox. See the note under the tables.

## Career arc

- **2007–08: debut at 16.** Fast-tracked into the national side in July 2007 and played all three formats within seven months. She was simultaneously a footballer: 18 caps for the Matildas (2007–13), including the 2011 FIFA Women's World Cup, making her the first person to appear at both a cricket and a football World Cup. Full-time cricket from about 2014.
- **2010–14: the T20 dynasty.** Player of the Final in the 2010 World T20 win (defended the last over), then titles again in 2012 and 2014. Won the 2013 ODI World Cup while playing on an injured ankle that needed surgery days after the final.
- **2015–17: all-round peak with the ball.** Match figures of 9/70 (including 6/32, her Test best) in the 2015 Ashes Test at Canterbury; Player of the Series in 2013-14 and 2015 Ashes campaigns. Won the ICC's Rachael Heyhoe Flint Award (Women's Cricketer of the Year) in 2017 and the Belinda Clark Award in 2016 and 2018.
- **November 2017: 213\*.** Her maiden Test century, in the day-night Ashes Test at North Sydney Oval, is still the highest Test score by an Australian woman (Annabel Sutherland's 210 in 2024 is second).
- **2018–19: the batting peak.** Dropped down Australia's T20I order in 2018, she answered with 777 runs in WBBL|04 (still the record for a WBBL season) and was Player of the Tournament. In 2019: 7/22 v England at Canterbury, her maiden ODI century, the first player of either sex to 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in T20Is, a third Ashes Player of the Series, a second Heyhoe Flint Award, ICC ODI Cricketer of the Year, and a third Belinda Clark Award (2020, for 2019).
- **2020: hamstring.** Tore a hamstring fielding in the T20 World Cup semi-final and missed the MCG final; surgery kept her out for about six months. Named a Wisden Cricketer of the Year (2020) and, in December 2020, ICC Women's Cricketer of the Decade across formats.
- **2021–22: milestones and a T20 demotion.** First woman to 5,000 international runs and 300 wickets (October 2021). Became the leading run-scorer and wicket-taker in Women's Ashes history during the 2021-22 series. Left out of the T20I XI in January 2022 as the selectors wanted faster scoring, and carried drinks for much of the 2022 Commonwealth Games (gold). Won the 2022 ODI World Cup in New Zealand despite missing two games with back spasms.
- **2023–24: rebuilt as a T20 batter.** Part of the 2023 T20 World Cup win (her sixth), batted at No. 3 on the 2023 Ashes tour, then dominated WPL 2024: the title with RCB, the Orange Cap (347 runs at 69.40) and 6/15 v Mumbai Indians, the best figures in WPL history. Australia lost the 2024 T20 World Cup semi-final.
- **2025: still central, captaincy handed on.** A quiet Ashes (Australia won 16-0 on points). At the ODI World Cup Australia went through the group unbeaten; she retired hurt with cramp on 32 against India and came back to hit the winning six in a then-record chase, then made 77 in the semi-final that India won with a record chase of their own. Handed the Sixers captaincy to Ashleigh Gardner before WBBL|11, then made 412 runs including a career-best 111 and passed 5,000 WBBL runs (only Beth Mooney had done so); named in the WBBL Team of the Tournament. Withdrew from WPL 2026 for personal reasons.
- **2026: Test record, seventh T20 World Cup.** A quad strain ruled her out of the India ODIs in February, but she made 76 in the Perth Test in March to pass 1,000 Test runs and Karen Rolton's Australian record. Toured the West Indies (6-0 sweep). At the T20 World Cup in England she was Australia's leading scorer going into the final (185 runs at 46.25, including 71 v Pakistan and a Player-of-the-Match 56 v India), retired hurt in the semi-final with a quad problem, and was 13* when Australia beat England by seven wickets at Lord's on 5 July — a record seventh T20 World Cup for Australia and her ninth World Cup winner's medal, a first for any cricketer. Named in the ICC Team of the Tournament. Since then: 223 runs at 55.75 in The Hundred, 65* in the Bangladesh ODIs, 52 off 30 in the first NZ T20I, and a hot start to WBBL|12.

## Playing style and technical profile

- **Batting method.** An orthodox, upright technique built on the straight and cover drive; ESPNcricinfo's 2017 World Cup coverage described the high-elbowed drive as her trademark, scoring mostly "in the V" and rotating strike with late dabs. She anchors rather than attacks in the longer formats: an ODI strike rate of 78.75 with 38 fifties against 3 hundreds (her first ODI century came in 2019, in her 12th year), and a Test strike rate of 46.81.
- **Conversion and tempo are the real criticisms.** The 38-to-3 fifties-to-hundreds ratio in ODIs is the clearest weakness in an otherwise exceptional record. Her T20 tempo was the other: a WBBL strike rate of 91.32 in 2021-22 was the slowest of that season's top 30 scorers and cost her a T20I place in January 2022 (ESPNcricinfo: "no longer considered a first-choice inclusion in the format").
- **The T20 rebuild.** She has said T20 batting was the part of her game that took longest to develop. From WBBL|08 (408 runs at a strike rate of 119, her first run-a-ball season since WBBL|04) she added range-hitting and a "360" game; her WBBL career strike rate now stands at 111.61 and she scored at 138 across the 2026 T20 World Cup group stage. Her T20I average (31.19) and strike rate (119.09) remain solid rather than dominant for a top-order batter, and she still has no T20I hundred (HS 75).
- **Bowling.** Right-arm fast-medium from a classical action, bowling mainly outswing to the right-hander. In 2017 Matthew Hoggard adjusted her delivery stride so her front foot pointed down the pitch rather than landing parallel to the crease, which she credited with more pace and swing. Wisden measured an average of 1.53 degrees of swing per ball, both ways, in the first five overs of her 7/22 at Canterbury in 2019. In T20s she has described holding the seam upright for bouncers and going cross-seam when the pitch offers movement. Her T20I bowling average (18.54) and economy (5.84) remain elite marks across the whole T20I era.
- **Bowling workload has been rationed since 2020.** After the 2020 hamstring surgery and the 2022 back trouble, she bowled only five overs in 12 matches in the 20 months to June 2026, then 2/14 in three overs v Bangladesh and two wickets in a single over v Pakistan at the 2026 World Cup. She has one wicket in her last six ODIs. Functionally she is now a top-order batter who can bowl a few overs of swing when conditions suit.
- **Fielding.** An outstanding outfielder and boundary catcher: 67 WBBL catches, the most by a non-keeper in the competition's history, plus 59 in ODIs and 51 in T20Is.
- **Temperament.** The biggest performances have come on the biggest stages: the last over of the 2010 final, 213* in an Ashes Test, 7/22 and 9/70 in Ashes matches, 77 in a World Cup semi-final, Player of the Match against India at Lord's in 2026. Cricket.com.au's phrase in June 2026 — she "often lifts when the stakes are highest" — is borne out by nine World Cup wins.
- **Injury pattern.** Ankle (2013), hamstring (2020), back (2022), hip (2025 Ashes), cramp and quad strains (2025–26). She keeps returning, but the recurrence of soft-tissue problems in her mid-30s is the obvious risk to her remaining career.

## Records and milestones

- **213\* v England, North Sydney Oval, November 2017** — highest Test score by an Australian woman (still stands; Sutherland 210 in 2024 is next) and third-highest in women's Tests behind Kiran Baluch (242) and Mithali Raj (214).
- **Australia's leading Test run-scorer** — passed Karen Rolton's 1,002 during her 76 at the WACA on 7 March 2026; 11th woman to 1,000 Test runs. Among women with more Test runs, only Enid Bakewell (59.88) averages more than her 59.17.
- **Only the second woman, after Charlotte Edwards, with 1,000 runs in each of Tests, ODIs and T20Is** (completed March 2026).
- **First cricketer to 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in T20Is** (2019); fourth woman to 2,000 runs and 100 wickets in T20Is (2024).
- **First woman to 5,000 international runs and 300 international wickets** (October 2021).
- **7/22 v England, Canterbury, July 2019** — held the record for the best ODI figures by an Australian woman from 2019 until Alana King's 7/18 at the 2025 World Cup.
- **Leading run-scorer and wicket-taker in Women's Ashes history** (passed Charlotte Edwards' runs and Katherine Sciver-Brunt's wickets in January 2022; the latest figures I found quote 1,552 runs and 71 wickets, which are dated and may be slightly below her current totals).
- **Nine World Cup titles** (ODI 2013, 2022; T20 2010, 2012, 2014, 2018, 2020, 2023, 2026) — the first cricketer of either sex to win nine; plus Commonwealth Games gold (2022).
- **WBBL:** record for most runs in a season (777 in WBBL|04, 2018-19, listed as 778 on the Wikipedia records page); highest batting average in the competition's history; most catches by a fielder (67); the highest partnership in WBBL history, 199* with Alyssa Healy v Melbourne Stars, Perth, 3 November 2019; second to Beth Mooney on career runs; 32 Player-of-the-Match awards; Player of the Tournament WBBL|04 and WBBL|10; titles in 2016-17 and 2017-18.
- **WPL:** 6/15 v Mumbai Indians, Delhi, 12 March 2024 — the best figures in WPL history and the only six-for; 2024 Orange Cap (347 runs at 69.40); most fifties in the WPL (8, as of February 2026); WPL champion 2024.
- **Awards:** Rachael Heyhoe Flint Award (ICC Women's Cricketer of the Year) 2017 and 2019; ICC Women's ODI Cricketer of the Year 2019; ICC Women's Cricketer of the Decade 2011–20; Wisden Cricketer of the Year 2020; Wisden Leading Woman Cricketer in the World 2016 and 2019; one of Wisden's Five Cricketers of the Decade; Belinda Clark Award 2016, 2018, 2020; Women's Ashes Player of the Series 2013-14, 2015, 2019; ICC Team of the Tournament, 2026 T20 World Cup; WBBL Team of the Tournament 2025-26.

## Recent form and current context (November 2025 – November 2026)

- **WBBL|11 (Nov–Dec 2025):** 412 runs, fourth in the competition, with 77* off 44 v Sydney Thunder and 111 off 71 v Adelaide Strikers (7 Dec, career best, 5,000-run milestone). The Sixers finished second and lost the Challenger to Perth by 11 runs; Hobart won the title.
- **WPL 2026 (Jan–Feb):** Withdrew for personal reasons despite RCB retaining her; played the Super Smash for Wellington instead. A September 2026 report (IANS, single source) says she has made herself available to RCB for WPL 2027.
- **India in Australia (Feb–Mar 2026):** Played the three T20Is (her 350th international came in Adelaide on 21 Feb; India won the series 2-1), then was ruled out of the ODIs with a quad strain. Returned for the day-night Test in Perth (6–8 Mar): 76 in a 128-run stand with Sutherland, Australia won by 10 wickets.
- **West Indies tour (Mar–Apr 2026):** Australia swept 3-0 in both formats; 42 off 28 in the second T20I and 1/0 in the third. Sophie Molineux captained, with Tahlia McGrath standing in for two ODIs.
- **T20 World Cup, England (Jun–Jul 2026):** Australia won all seven matches. Perry: 2/14 v Bangladesh, 71 off 48 and two wickets in one over v Pakistan, 56 off 38 (Player of the Match) v India, retired hurt after seven balls of the semi-final chase with "quad awareness", passed fit and 13* in the final. 185 runs at 46.25 before the final, so 198 for the tournament. ICC Team of the Tournament; Beth Mooney was Player of the Tournament.
- **The Hundred (Jul–Aug 2026):** 223 runs at 55.75 for a struggling Birmingham Phoenix, including 68* off 46 v Welsh Fire and 62* off 39 in the last game v MI London.
- **Bangladesh in Australia (Oct 2026, Brisbane):** Australia won the ODIs 3-0 (McGrath captain); Perry 19* and 65* in the last two matches. The scheduled T20Is were dropped from the tour.
- **New Zealand T20Is (18–22 Oct 2026, North Sydney Oval):** 52 off 30, her 12th T20I fifty, in a two-wicket win; Australia won the series 2-0 with the third match rained off.
- **WBBL|12 (in progress):** Player of the Match for 58 off 38 v Brisbane Heat (30 Oct) and 84* off 57 plus 1/5 v Melbourne Stars (6 Nov, a one-run win); run out in an unusual third-umpire referral v Renegades (12 Nov, Sixers won); 150th WBBL match reached this week. The Sixers lead the table at 5-1 after six games.
- **ICC rankings:** No. 10 in the women's ODI batting rankings (664 points, as of 2 Nov 2026). Not in the top ten of the ODI all-rounder list or any T20I list (as of 9 Nov 2026).
- **Contracts and selection:** CA central contract 2026-27 (named April 2026); Sixers deal through WBBL|13; retained by Birmingham Phoenix; Australia's captaincy sits with Sophie Molineux in white-ball cricket after Alyssa Healy's retirement. In a CA interview this season she said she is in her 20th summer and "not yet thinking about life beyond the game". Next internationals: New Zealand at home, three T20Is from 21 Feb 2027 and three ODIs from 1 Mar 2027.

## Off-field and context

- **Captaincy record:** Sydney Sixers captain from WBBL|01 (2015-16) to WBBL|10 (2024-25), two titles; handed over to Ashleigh Gardner on 7 November 2025 and stayed on as a player. Captained Victoria briefly in 2021-22 and Birmingham Phoenix in 2024. Has never captained Australia on a permanent basis.
- **Football:** W-League with Central Coast Mariners, Canberra United and Sydney FC (2008–16); 18 Matildas caps and 3 goals; W-League Young Player of the Year 2009.
- **Injuries that shaped the career:** ankle surgery after the 2013 World Cup; hamstring surgery March 2020; back spasms at the 2022 World Cup; quad strain February 2026 and again at the 2026 T20 World Cup.
- **Personal:** Studied Economic and Social Sciences at the University of Sydney. Married rugby player Matt To'omua in December 2015; the couple separated in 2020. Author of the memoir *Perspective* (2019) and a children's book series. Supports the McGrath Foundation, Sporting Chance Cancer Foundation and the LBW Trust. Withdrew from WPL 2026 citing personal reasons; no further detail was published.
- **Honours:** Sport NSW Athlete of the Year 2019; Australia Post Legend of Cricket 2021.
