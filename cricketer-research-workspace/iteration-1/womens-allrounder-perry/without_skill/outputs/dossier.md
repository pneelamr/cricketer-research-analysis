The headline: she is the most-capped Australian woman in history, Australia's leading women's Test run-scorer, the first cricketer of either gender to reach 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in T20Is, and the holder of a record seven T20 World Cup winner's medals. At 36 she is still Australia's first-choice No. 3/4 in all formats and the Sixers' opener.

### Format notes

**Tests.** Last Test: v India, WACA Ground, Perth, 6-8 March 2026 (day-night), where her 76 off 116 balls (10 fours, 1 six; 128-run fourth-wicket stand with Annabel Sutherland, who made 129) carried her past Karen Rolton's 1,002 to become Australia's all-time leading women's Test run-scorer. That innings also made her the second woman after Charlotte Edwards to pass 1,000 runs in all three formats, and put her 10th on the all-time women's Test run list. Her 213* (v England, North Sydney Oval, November 2017) remains the highest Test score by an Australian woman and the second-highest in women's Test history behind Kiran Baluch's 242. She has two Test five-wicket hauls, with a best of 6/32. The 2025 Ashes Test at the MCG was a write-off (2 runs after a corked hip on day one), which is why her 2025-26 Test output was entirely the Perth innings.

**ODIs.** Last series: v Bangladesh at Allan Border Field, 9-14 October 2026 (Australia 3-0), where she made 110 runs across three innings including 65* in the third ODI. Her 7/22 v England at Canterbury (July 2019) is the best ODI bowling by an Australian and the second-best in women's ODI history (Sajjida Shah's 7/4 v Japan, 2003). Three ODI hundreds, the best known being 112* v England at Canterbury in the 2019 Ashes, the same match as the 7/22. ODI World Cup record through the 2025 semi-final: 32 matches, 848 runs at 49.88, seven fifties. She has bowled far less in ODIs since 2022; the 167th wicket came only in 2026.

**T20Is.** Last series: v New Zealand at North Sydney Oval, 18-22 October 2026 (Australia 2-0, third match abandoned); 52 off 30 in the first game (her 12th T20I fifty) rescued a chase that Australia won by two wickets. T20 World Cup career: first player to 50 appearances (51 in all), 630 runs at 30, and a record seven titles (2010, 2012, 2014, 2018, 2020, 2023, 2026). She was Player of the Final in 2010 (3/18 v New Zealand, Bridgetown). Her 2020 home World Cup ended with a torn hamstring in the group game v New Zealand; she missed the MCG final and had surgery.

## 5. Records and milestones

International
- Australia's most-capped woman (passed the record in October 2021, 252nd match); first Australian woman to 350 internationals (3rd T20I v India, Adelaide, 21 Feb 2026); now on 370.
- Australia's leading women's Test run-scorer (1,006; passed Rolton 7 Mar 2026). Second woman, after Charlotte Edwards, with 1,000-plus runs in Tests, ODIs and T20Is.
- 213* v England, 2017: highest Test score by an Australian woman; second-highest in women's Tests.
- 329 runs between dismissals in Tests (2017-2019), a world record at the time.
- 7/22 v England, Canterbury, 2019: best ODI figures by an Australian; second-best in women's ODIs.
- First cricketer of either gender to 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in T20Is (28 Jul 2019).
- First woman to 5,000 runs and 300 wickets in international cricket (Oct 2021); now 8,255 runs and 337 wickets.
- Third bowler to 150 ODI wickets (Sept 2019).
- Seven T20 World Cup titles, the most by any player; first to 50 T20 World Cup appearances; equal-most T20 World Cup finals (with Alyssa Healy).
- Player of the Final at the 2010 T20 World Cup (3/18) and the 2013 ODI World Cup (3/19 v West Indies, Mumbai).

WBBL and franchise
- Second to 5,000 WBBL runs; first to 700 in a WBBL season (778, 2018-19).
- WPL Orange Cap 2024; best bowling figures in WPL history (6/15).

Dual sport
- First Australian to play in both an ICC World Cup and a FIFA World Cup (2011).

## 7. Career arc

**2007-2012: the prodigy.** ODI debut at 16 in July 2007; Matildas debut two weeks later. Test and T20I debuts in early 2008. Bowled Australia to the 2010 T20 World Cup (Player of the Final) and starred again in 2012.

**2013-2016: all-rounder of the world.** 3/19 in the 2013 World Cup final, then ankle surgery. Committed solely to cricket from 2014. Ashes Player of the Series in 2015. First Belinda Clark Award in 2016.

**2017-2019: peak.** Inaugural ICC Women's Cricketer of the Year (2017). 213* at North Sydney Oval in the 2017-18 Ashes. The 2019 Ashes tour produced 7/22 and 112* in the same ODI, a 6/32 in the Test, and her third Player of the Series award; she finished 2019 as ICC Cricketer and ODI Cricketer of the Year. The 2018-19 WBBL season (778 runs) remains one of the great T20 batting seasons.

**2020-2022: setback and reinvention.** Hamstring tear in the 2020 home T20 World Cup and surgery. Then the decade awards. A heavier, lower-strike-rate T20 period followed, during which her role shifted from new-ball bowler to specialist batter; from 2022 she rebuilt her power game, and her WBBL strike rate jumped from about 103 to above 130. World Cup and Commonwealth Games winner in 2022.

**2023-2025: the batter.** T20 World Cup 2023; WPL title and Orange Cap 2024; 2025 Ashes 16-0 (although her own Test was lost to injury). At the 2025 ODI World Cup in India she made 47* v India in Visakhapatnam despite retiring hurt mid-innings and returning, and 77 off 88 in the semi-final defeat to India in Navi Mumbai.

**2025-26: still accumulating.** WBBL|11 century and the 5,000-run mark. Skipped WPL 2026 for personal reasons; played Super Smash instead. February 2026: 350th international; a quad injury then cost her the India ODIs. March: the Perth Test record (76), in what was also Alyssa Healy's farewell match. Late March-April: 6-0 white-ball sweep in the Caribbean (77 ODI runs, 96 T20I runs). June-July: an unbeaten T20 World Cup campaign in England under Sophie Molineux. Perry made 198 runs at 49.50 (SR about 133, top score 71 v Pakistan at Headingley, where she also took two wickets) and 4 wickets at 7.50 (economy 6.00, best 2/9), retired hurt with a quad problem in the semi-final v West Indies, passed fit for the final and was 13* when Australia beat England by seven wickets at Lord's on 5 July (153/3 in 17.1 overs chasing 151). Wisden rated her 9/10 for the tournament. August: 223 runs at 55.75 in The Hundred. October: 110 runs v Bangladesh (65*), 52 off 30 v New Zealand. Now mid-WBBL|12.

**What's next.** WBBL|12 finals (Sixers currently top); the inaugural Women's Champions Trophy in Sri Lanka, 14-28 Feb 2027, which she has publicly said she is targeting; the 2027 Ashes in England; contracted to the Sixers through WBBL|13. No retirement announcement of any kind has been made; her recent interviews describe a "Federer-like" appetite to keep improving.

## 8. Football career

Primarily a defender/midfielder. Debut v Hong Kong on 4 August 2007, aged 16, scoring in the second minute. 18 caps, 3 goals (2007-2012): the debut goal, one v South Korea at the 2008 Asian Cup, and Australia's only goal in the 3-1 quarter-final loss to Sweden at the 2011 World Cup (9 July 2011), having come on as a substitute v Norway earlier in that tournament. W-League: Central Coast Mariners (2008-09), Canberra United (2009-12, 2011-12 champions), Sydney FC (2012-16); 45 league appearances, 3 goals; Young Player of the Year 2009. Several seasons of double-booked finals (she repeatedly chose cricket) ended with her omission from the Matildas squad in March 2014 and a last W-League match on 13 December 2015. Some sources list 16 caps; Football Australia's 18 is used here.

---

## 9. Off the field

Studied Economic and Social Sciences at the University of Sydney. Married rugby union international Matt To'omua in December 2015; they separated in 2020. Author of the memoir *Perspective* (2019) and a children's book series; long-standing ambassador for the McGrath Foundation and the LBW Trust.
